Neptune Insurance Holdings Insurance · US S1 — Velocity

On the Q2 2026 call (22 Jul 2026) CEO Trevor Burgess said the newly live quote-to-bind model is "definitely having a positive impact on the new business sales"; ~3,700 agents exchanged 33,000 messages with the Atlas+ assistant during the quarter. Q2 revenue $55.9m (+33% YoY), written premium $126.9m (+31%), lifetime written loss ratio 19.5%, FY26 revenue guided to $199m. None of the revenue is attributed to either system.

Mechanism
Two distinct systems in the revenue path. (1) A machine-learning quote-to-bind model inside Triton, the underwriting platform, scoring and routing submissions with no human underwriter in the loop. (2) Atlas+, an agent-facing assistant sitting on the quote screen answering the writing agent's questions in real time and drafting follow-up email, with a ranked next-best-task feed due in Q3. The stated route to revenue is higher agent quoting throughput and a better quote-to-bind conversion rate.
Why this shape
S1, not S2 or S3. Every disclosed number is an input metric - agent count, message count - and the revenue statement is qualitative. Management gave a precise written-premium figure ($126.9m) and a precise FY guide ($199m) and attributed neither to the AI: this is the S1 conversion silence in its purest form, from a company that is otherwise unusually forthcoming. The S3 read was run and failed: >55% of new business outside mandatory flood zones and >75% voluntary purchase looks like new demand, but NFIP already writes voluntary policies outside the SFHA, so test (b) fails on prior alternative; and Burgess said flatly "We're not using AI to set pricing... All of the AI that we're using is algorithmic," which fails test (c) on the capability gate. Appendix, not main, because the subject is out of scope in its own words - the 21 Jul release calls Neptune an "AI-native managing general agent" that "operates without human underwriters." It does not test whether an ordinary incumbent gets revenue ROI from AI spend; it tests whether an AI-native entrant can take share in a boring line. Logged so that comparator stays visible rather than being discarded.

The finding that matters here is the denominator: two-thirds of genuinely deep enterprise AI adoption sits inside the technology sector, i.e. inside the population METHOD excludes as subjects.
Why this shape
Counter-evidence, tagged to S2 because S2 is where the bulk of revenue-attribution claims live, but it cuts against S3 equally. A J-curve in profitability with no measured productivity or capex difference is a margin story, not a top-line story; the paper finds no top-line effect to report. Directly bears on why this log's positive column is empty: the non-tech incumbents it tracks are precisely the slow-adopting tail, so scarce revenue disclosure may reflect thin real adoption rather than adoption without payoff. Those are very different worlds and this study does not separate them.
