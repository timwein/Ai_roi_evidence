Honeywell International Industrials / Industrial automation · US S2 — Share

On the Q2 2026 call (23 Jul 2026) CEO Vimal Kapur guided software ARR growth of ~15% for 2026 and said the business will "inch from about $900 million to a little over $1 billion of ARR in that segment this year." He described Forge as "the primary mechanism for delivering 'physical AI' to customers" and named software ARR as "the best way to measure our progress" toward autonomy. No revenue was attributed to AI itself.

Mechanism
Honeywell Forge is the connected-plant software layer sold on top of Honeywell's installed automation base (Kapur: "we have been selling automation since 1975"). It is sold as subscription ARR, and the AI/autonomy content is being positioned as the reason customers move up the subscription tiers. The revenue mechanism is software attach onto existing hardware relationships, not a new product with no predecessor.
Why this shape
S2. Test: absent this AI capability, would the buyer have bought something similar from somebody? Yes - industrial asset-performance and process-optimization software is a contested category (AVEVA, Siemens, GE Vernova, ABB, Rockwell). Growth here is share taken inside an existing budget line, zero-sum at industry level and GDP-neutral. Explicitly NOT S3: Forge has a predecessor in Honeywell's own catalog going back years, and no capability gate at the 24-month frontier is established. The attribution weakness is the point of logging it: this is the largest AI-linked top-line number a non-tech industrial put on the record this quarter, and management still did not attribute a single dollar of it to AI - only to "Forge offerings" and "the launch of new software products." 'Physical AI' is the framing wrapped around a pre-existing software line.

Grant Thornton 2026 AI Impact Survey - manufacturing cut (n=100 of 950) Manufacturing / Cross-sector · US S2 — Share⚠ Counter-evidence

Survey of 950 business leaders across 10 industries, fielded 23 Feb - 18 Mar 2026: "Among manufacturing respondents, zero reported significant revenue uplift from AI initiatives, compared to 12% of executives across all industries. Zero reported significant cost savings, again compared to 12% overall." 47% of manufacturers reported only minimal revenue uplift; 64% reported increased efficiency. Manufacturers led all sectors in operational AI deployment (62%) while reporting the weakest financial return.

Mechanism
Not a deployment finding - a base-rate measurement of the population this log samples from, and the cleanest available quantification of the efficiency-without-revenue gap: 64% report efficiency gains, 0% report significant revenue uplift, in the sector with the HIGHEST operational deployment rate. If efficiency mechanically converted to top line, the sector leading deployment should not be the sector reporting zero.
Why this shape
Counter-evidence, tagged to S2 because S2 (attributed incremental revenue on existing products) is the shape most non-tech corporates claim and the shape this null result most directly contradicts. It also constrains S3 by implication: a sector reporting zero significant revenue uplift is not opening new markets. The 58%-vs-15% headline elsewhere in the same survey (fully-integrated vs piloting firms reporting AI-driven revenue growth) is a selection-effect artifact, not causal evidence - firms that integrate deeply are firms that already saw something work - and should not be cited as offsetting the manufacturing zero.

The signal: Albertsons

Companies repeat numbers that are working. The disclosure asymmetry — specific quantified cost and productivity metrics from BofA, specific quantified capex from Albertsons, and qualitative adjectives everywhere the revenue line should be — is the actual pattern across every non-tech Q2 print read today, and it is what this log exists to measure.

On its Q1 FY2026 call (23 Jul 2026) Albertsons reaffirmed AI as "foundational" and guided FY26 capex of $1.9-2.0bn toward store modernization and AI capabilities, but disclosed no AI-attributed revenue metric. The 10% Ask AI basket-size lift CEO Susan Morris put on the record on the 7 Jan 2026 call was not repeated. Digital sales grew 13%, not attributed to AI.

Mechanism
Ask AI conversational search in the Albertsons/Safeway/Vons/Jewel-Osco mobile apps surfaces complementary items mid-search, expanding the basket; household-level personalized offers on top of ~tens of millions of loyalty households. The mechanism is real and the January number was specific; what is missing is any repeat measurement.
Why this shape
S2, and counter-evidence to it. Basket expansion inside an existing customer base buying existing groceries in existing stores - absent the AI, these shoppers buy groceries from somebody. Zero-sum at industry level, GDP-neutral. Logged as counter-evidence because the only quantified revenue-side AI metric this company had ever disclosed vanished from the next disclosure while AI capex was reaffirmed and expanded. Four named enterprise AI priorities were given (digital CX, merchandising intelligence, labor optimization, supply chain optimization); three of the four are cost-side, and the revenue-side one carried no number.

S&P 500 (510 firms, 2016-2025) - MIT FutureTech / CMU study Cross-sector · US S2 — Share⚠ Counter-evidence

Peer-style study of AI adoption in 510 S&P 500 firms, scored from SEC 10-K language: in 2025 only 11% had AI "deeply integrated into their business processes", plus a further 10% "using AI in the production of goods and delivery of services", up from 5% in 2022. The technology sector "accounts for two-thirds of deeply integrated enterprise adoption", with only "slowly accelerating adoption among non-technology firms". Profitability follows a J-curve from no adoption to deep adoption, but the authors "observe no differences in capex or productivity".

Mechanism
Not a deployment finding - a measurement of the population this log samples from. Adoption is scored off 10-K language, which sits under liability for materially false statements, so it is a harder measure than survey self-report. The finding that matters here is the denominator: two-thirds of genuinely deep enterprise AI adoption sits inside the technology sector, i.e. inside the population METHOD excludes as subjects.
Why this shape
Counter-evidence, tagged to S2 because S2 is where the bulk of revenue-attribution claims live, but it cuts against S3 equally. A J-curve in profitability with no measured productivity or capex difference is a margin story, not a top-line story; the paper finds no top-line effect to report. Directly bears on why this log's positive column is empty: the non-tech incumbents it tracks are precisely the slow-adopting tail, so scarce revenue disclosure may reflect thin real adoption rather than adoption without payoff. Those are very different worlds and this study does not separate them.
