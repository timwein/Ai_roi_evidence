The disclosure asymmetry has stopped being a pattern and started being a rule. Four consecutive weeks now: quantified AI numbers from non-tech incumbents are cost metrics, adoption metrics, or forward targets. Lloyds can put GBP 2bn of savings on a slide out to 2030 and cannot put a number on the revenue growth it says AI will drive in the same sentence. Admiral can tell you that four-fifths of its code is machine-written and nothing about what that saved.
New this run: the first case of a revenue attribution being withdrawn rather than never given. Amazon had the single largest AI-attributed revenue number in public markets - ~$12bn of incremental annualized sales on its shopping assistant - and this quarter replaced it with "spend 40% more per order", a correlation that cannot survive selection bias. Companies repeat numbers that are working. Watch whether it comes back in Q3.
