General Motors Automotive · US S2 — Share

On the Q2 2026 call (21 Jul 2026) CFO Paul Jacobson said "Our OnStar digital business, including Super Cruise, remains a growing margin accretive asset", that "second quarter recognized revenue was $800 million, up 20% year-over-year", that "subscriber growth drove deferred revenue to $6.3 billion, up almost 50% from a year ago", and gave "estimated full-year 2026 realized revenue of more than $3 billion" at "margins on OnStar around 70%". CEO Mary Barra said GM "achieved our best quarter and first half ever for the new Super Cruise-equipped vehicles" and estimated "160,000 incremental Super Cruise units" from making it standard on high-end Silverado and Sierra trims; Jacobson put attach rates "in the 30%-40% range". GM's Q2 slide deck, as rendered by Investing.com, additionally puts Super Cruise on pace for approximately $400m of full-year 2026 revenue on recognized revenue up ~70% year-over-year, with 850,000 subscribers expected by year-end - those three figures were NOT verifiable in a GM-hosted document this run (investor.gm.com returned 503 on both the deck and the shareholder letter) and are carried at Tier C.

Mechanism
Super Cruise is a neural-network hands-free highway driving system sold as a subscription attached to the vehicle, bundled free for an initial term and then converted to paid at a 30-40% attach rate. GM is now making it standard on high-end full-size pickup trims to widen the installed base that can later convert. It is reported inside OnStar, GM's connected-services subscription business, which is where the $800m quarterly and >$3bn annual figures come from.
Why this shape
S2, not S3, and the same reasoning as the Ford BlueCruise entry of 2026-08-01 - GM is its direct comparator. (a) Counterfactual purchase: a buyer who wants hands-free highway driving can buy Ford's BlueCruise or Tesla FSD, so the demand pre-exists and a rival lost the sale. (b) Prior alternative: a paid competitor product, not going without. (c) Capability gate: FAILS decisively - Super Cruise has shipped since 2017 and needs nothing that appeared in the last 24 months. (d) Newly economic: no evidence offered; the economics changed because GM changed trim packaging, not because a cost curve moved. LOAD-BEARING CAVEAT, same as Ford: GM does not call this AI, it calls it software and services - the AI characterisation is this log's, on the grounds that hands-free autonomy is a neural-network product. SECOND CAVEAT, and it is the bigger one: the verified dollar figures are OnStar-inclusive, and OnStar is mostly telematics, connectivity and safety services that predate modern AI entirely. The AI-specific slice is Super Cruise, and the only figure sizing it is the deck-sourced ~$400m, which is roughly an eighth of the >$3bn. Treat the $800m as the denominator, not the finding.

Falsifier
GM has never broken out Super Cruise revenue in its own audited or IR-hosted materials. If Q3 and Q4 2026 pass with the OnStar aggregate still the only disclosed dollar figure, and Super Cruise appearing only as subscriber counts and attach rates, then the AI-attributable revenue here is unmeasurable and this entry is a bookkeeping placeholder rather than evidence. Conversely, if Super Cruise gets its own revenue line and clears the ~$400m pace, the finding strengthens. Third check: the 30-40% attach rate is the load-bearing number - if it falls as the standard-fit base widens with the Silverado and Sierra packaging change, the 160,000 incremental units convert to far less revenue than the unit count implies.
