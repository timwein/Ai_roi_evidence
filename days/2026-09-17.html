AXA SA (AXA France) Insurance · France S2 — Share

In the AXA France appendix of the AXA Group Investor Day 2026 deck (15 Sep 2026), under the heading "Delivering 2024-2026 plan | AI for distribution", AXA discloses that its ARiANE programme - described as an "AI-powered commercial activation engine" "turning customer signals into 'next-best offers' and prioritized sales actions" - has "22 Target use cases activated in motor & home", a "+50% Digital leads conversion rate" and "+500k New contracts". On the group AI page the same result appears as "x1.5 conversion rate" from "Deployment of AI sales coach", footnoted "Achievement observed within the deployment scope in at least one entity where the deployment has been completed." No euro figure is attached to the 500,000 contracts and AXA does not say over what period they were written beyond the 2024-2026 plan.

Mechanism
ARiANE scores inbound and installed-base customer signals and pushes ranked next-best-offer actions to AXA France's agent and direct channels across 22 motor and home use cases. The revenue path is ordinary funnel arithmetic: the same lead volume converts at 1.5x, and the incremental conversions accumulate as written contracts. Nothing about the underlying motor or home product changes; the AI sits entirely in targeting and sales prioritisation.
Why this shape
S2, not S1, because the disclosed figure is an output - contracts written - not only the conversion rate that produced them. Who lost the sale: French motor insurance is compulsory and home insurance near-universal, so essentially every one of those 500,000 buyers would have bought a comparable policy from a competing French insurer or bancassurer. This is share-shifting inside a mature, saturated pool and is GDP-neutral at industry level. Not S3 on test (a) - the counterfactual purchase clearly exists; not on (b) - the prior alternative was a rival policy, not going without; not on (c) - next-best-offer propensity scoring is textbook pre-2024 ML. Note the caveat AXA itself prints: the x1.5 is "observed within the deployment scope in at least one entity", i.e. a best-case cut, not a group-wide average. This is nonetheless the hardest unit-count revenue attribution any non-tech incumbent has put in this log, and the first time a top-five global insurer has named a contract count next to an AI programme on a Tier A source.

RXO, Inc. Logistics · US S1 — Velocity

At Morgan Stanley's 14th Annual Laguna Conference on 16 Sep 2026, Chief Strategy Officer Jared Weisfeld said RXO "can grow volume by about 15% plus based on our current headcount and how we're leveraging our technology", with brokerage headcount down a mid-teens percentage year over year. RXO's own Q2 2026 earnings presentation, filed as a Form 8-K exhibit on 6 Aug 2026, quantifies the underlying agents: "5x more spot quote emails processed per day via proprietary AI agent", "Improved AI freight matching model and user experience helped increase digital carrier offers by ~25%", "~50% increase in agentic phone calls" and "~30% improvement in Managed Transportation workflow automation", under the header "Committed to technology and AI investments with strong returns".

Mechanism
A freight broker's revenue is gated by how many carrier and shipper quotes its people can touch. RXO's spot-quote agent reads and prices inbound quote emails, the freight-matching model surfaces loads to carriers digitally, and agentic voice handles routine carrier calls. Each removes a human touch from the quoting loop, so load volume decouples from brokerage headcount. RXO says users of the AI spot quote agent showed higher gross profit per load and truckload volume than non-users, and grew truckload volume 2% year over year in Q2 while headcount fell.
Why this shape
S1: every disclosed number is an input - quotes per day, carrier offers, agentic calls, volume headroom on fixed headcount. Revenue conversion is partially disclosed and partially absent: RXO reports an 11% sequential rise in truckload gross profit per load, but attributes it to a 900bps sequential increase in spot mix, not to AI, and gives no AI-attributed revenue or gross-profit figure anywhere. The one causal revenue claim - that agent users outperform non-users on gross profit per load and volume - is made without a magnitude, which is exactly the shape of claim METHOD's inclusion bar rejects on its own. It is logged only because the input metrics beneath it are Tier A and quantified.

C.H. Robinson Worldwide, Inc. Logistics · US S1 — Velocity

In a newsroom post dated 10 Sep 2026 summarising its appearances at Citi's Global TMT Conference (8 Sep) and the Jefferies Global Industrials Conference (9 Sep), C.H. Robinson states that "since late 2022, productivity in C.H. Robinson's North American Surface Transportation and Global Forwarding businesses has improved by more than 60%" and that "we've had 13 quarters in a row of truckload outgrowth and 10 consecutive quarters of beating EPS consensus", on AI token spend of "less than $1.2 million" annually: "There is no hobby spend on AI at C.H. Robinson. Every dollar we spend on tokens, every dollar we spend on engineering capacity has a readymade high-probability ROI assigned to it." The underlying quoting metric, given at both conferences, is that the transactional-quote agent lifted response coverage from roughly 60-65% of the ~600,000 annual North American quote requests to 100%, at 31-32 seconds versus 17-20 minutes, with headroom to 6 million quotes a year without extra personnel.

Mechanism
Freight brokerage revenue is capped by quote coverage: a quote never answered is freight never priced. C.H. Robinson's agent answers the whole inbound transactional-quote queue rather than the two-thirds a human desk could reach, which is why CFO Damon Lee frames it as reach rather than cost - "I'm now getting to a third of the universe of freight that was available to me before that I never got to." A second agent re-prices continuously rather than on a 30-90 day cycle, which the company says lifted repricing acceptance to 93% from roughly 50%.
Why this shape
S1: the numbers are coverage, latency and capacity - inputs on a revenue-producing process - not attributed revenue. Revenue conversion is claimed but not quantified: the company links Lean AI to 13 straight quarters of truckload outgrowth, i.e. share gain, without isolating how much of that outgrowth the agents produced. Note the ledger call - the same programme carries a genuine cost story (gross profit per employee up 60%, North American surface transportation headcount down ~30%) and is logged once, here, because the entry is about quote coverage, not headcount. Age noted: the disclosures are 7-9 days old, outside the trailing 48 hours, and are logged as a strong unlogged finding from the past two weeks.

Bamboo Insurance Services, Inc. Insurance · US S1 — Velocity

In an amended Form S-1 filed 14 Sep 2026, the California homeowners MGU Bamboo states: "For the year ended December 31, 2025, an average of 88% of our policies converted from quote to bind through straight-through processing without manual intervention", that it delivers "over 75% of quotes that can be bound as of February 2026", and that its platform produces "approximately 85% straight-through processing, bindable quotes in less than five minutes, which we believe far outperforms industry norms". Over the same period Managed Premium "grew to $766 million from $484 million", a 58% increase, MGU segment revenue grew 68%, and Attritional Claims Frequency fell from 4.0% in Q1 2023 to 2.0% in Q2 2026.

Mechanism
Bamboo scores each submission against "over 200 granular datapoints" at address level, including an "AI-powered platform that converts aerial imagery into true roof-condition insights" that replaces roof-age proxies, then routes the risk to whichever of its programmes can take it. Because aggregation is managed in real time, the platform can return a bindable quote instead of declining after an agent has already invested time - which is the actual revenue mechanism, since a declined submission is a lost sale and a lost agent relationship.
Why this shape
Appendix on scope, consistent with the Neptune (28 Jul) and Orion180 (13 Sep) precedents: Bamboo is a technology-first insurance entrant, not an incumbent deploying AI, and describes itself as "a tech- and data-enabled MGU". S1, not S3, despite the tempting setting. Test (b) fails: California homeowners who could not get private cover had the FAIR Plan, and Bamboo's own DIC products are sold explicitly as a wrap around it - a prior alternative existed. Test (c) fails harder: aerial-imagery roof scoring and CAT aggregation modelling are not capabilities that appeared in the last 24 months. What Bamboo actually has is superior risk selection into a market incumbents cannot rebalance because California restricts non-renewals - a regulatory arbitrage amplified by analytics, not new demand. Revenue conversion is disclosed in the same document (58% premium growth) but Bamboo attributes it to the technology platform in general, never isolating the AI component.

AXA SA Insurance · France C4 — Non-tech · non-engineering

At its Investor Day on 15 Sep 2026 AXA launched its 2027-2029 plan "Growing Forward" and put a number on AI for the first time: "Scaling AI across the value chain, generating EUR 500-700m recurring value by 2029", footnoted "Expected, pre-tax, allocated within the three pillars of the plan and net of investments." The figure is repeated on the plan summary page as "EUR 500-700m recurring AI value from 2029" alongside topline growth of ">5% p.a. CAGR 2026-29e", a P&C combined ratio of "ca. 91%" and an expense-ratio improvement of "ca. -90bps". AXA also discloses "80% of employees using AI regularly" and "~70% AI-ready core insurance systems".

Mechanism
AXA allocates the AI benefit across its three plan pillars - growth, technical excellence and efficiency - naming an AI sales coach, end-to-end AI call and email answering, the Photon pricing engine, an AI underwriting cockpit, AI fraud detection and claims-recovery automation. The stated cost mechanisms are a ca. -90bps non-commission expense ratio and a -40bps loss ratio from claims-cost savings by 2029.
Why this shape
C4 - non-tech, non-engineering - but flagged hard on METHOD trap 3: "recurring value" is a company-defined composite. AXA explicitly says it is "allocated within the three pillars", one of which is Growth, so the number mixes revenue uplift, loss-ratio improvement and expense reduction and AXA does not split it. It is also net of investments, which is better practice than most - the run-cost offset (trap 6) is at least nominally handled. Logged here rather than in the revenue ledger because the headline number is a value construct, not attributed revenue; the revenue-shaped part of the same investor day is logged separately as the AXA France ARiANE entry, which is a distinct disclosure with its own figures.
