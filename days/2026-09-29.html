KPMG International - 'Unlocking AI value in insurance: A leader's guide to AI-enabled transformation' (insurance leaders, 20 countries and six regions, organisations with 500+ employees; respondent count not disclosed) Insurance / Cross-sector research · Global S2 — Share⚠ Counter-evidence

KPMG research published 29 Sep 2026, fielded 20-29 May 2026 among insurance leaders in 20 countries and six regions at organisations with 500 or more employees, reports that "only 5% to 10%" of insurers' AI budgets "is allocated to new products or revenue-generating opportunities", against "nearly half of insurance budgets" directed to "operational and back-office efficiency"; that "71% use AI mainly for content generation or routine task automation"; that only "one-quarter (25%)" are "using it to drive growth through new products, services and AI-enabled offerings"; and that "only 11% describe their view of AI return on investment as very clear, while 23% report limited clarity or no clear view". The same research reports "77% of insurance leaders believe failing to respond to AI could undermine competitiveness within five years".

Mechanism
No mechanism - this is a budget-allocation measurement, and that is the point. It reports where insurers are actually pointing AI money: back-office efficiency roughly half, new products and revenue-generating opportunities 5-10%. The mechanisms this log spends most of its search effort hunting in insurance (new risk classes, newly priceable exposures, AI-enabled SKUs with no catalogue predecessor) are, on this reading, funded out of a single-digit share of the sector's AI spend.

Counter-evidence, tagged to S2 by the log's established convention for population-level survey nulls (MIT FutureTech 2026-07-26, McKinsey 2026-08-28, BCG 2026-09-02), because S2 is where the bulk of revenue-attribution claims sit. It also sits in unresolved tension with two entries logged in the last eight days: Grant Thornton's insurance cut (2026-09-22, 52% of insurance leaders reporting AI-attributable revenue growth) and Accenture's 263-executive survey (2026-09-28, 81% claiming at least 5% more gross written premium from AI). GFT Technologies / Wakefield Research - 'The Future of AI, Unfiltered' (945 CIOs and CTOs, 19 countries, companies with $500m+ annual revenue) Cross-sector research · Global S2 — Share⚠ Counter-evidence

Survey of 945 CIOs and CTOs across 19 countries at companies with at least $500m in annual revenue, fielded 11-31 Aug 2026 by Wakefield Research for GFT Technologies and published 29 Sep 2026: "84% say limitations in their legacy systems have caused their organization to cancel an AI pilot or project"; "89% are concerned that global AI investment may be growing faster than the business value it can realistically deliver"; and "91% believe some public companies cite AI to justify workforce changes that are primarily intended to boost their share price".

Mechanism
None - a negative finding about the pipeline that feeds every shape in this log. The 84% is behavioural: projects that existed and were killed, at large companies, for an infrastructural reason. If it holds anywhere near that level, the gap between announced AI initiatives and deployed ones is wider than the announcement flow suggests, and a large share of what this log searches for was cancelled before it could produce a number.

Counter-evidence tagged to S2, the shape that claims attributed financial impact on existing business, following the convention used for prior cross-sector survey nulls. That is belief rather than evidence of the practice, and it cannot promote or demote any C-ledger entry on its own, but it is the first time the trap has been measured rather than asserted. The 89% is the capex-versus-realised-revenue gap restated as sentiment.
