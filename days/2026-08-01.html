Ford Motor Company Automotive · US S2 — Share

On the Q2 2026 call (28 Jul 2026) CEO Jim Farley said BlueCruise "paid subscriptions in Q2 grew by 20%" and that "BlueCruise made up 50% of our retail Integrated Services revenue"; Ford reported 1.6m total paid subscriptions, including 900,000+ Ford Pro Intelligence subscriptions "growing over 20% year-over-year".

Mechanism
BlueCruise is a machine-learning-based hands-free highway driving system sold as a standalone subscription ($49.99/month, $495/year, $2,495 one-time) on top of the vehicle. Revenue is the subscription itself, attached at or after point of sale by dealers; Farley noted "our dealers are getting better at selling" it. Ford Pro Intelligence is the equivalent telematics/fleet-software subscription on the commercial side.
Why this shape
S2, not S3. Test (a) counterfactual purchase: a buyer wanting hands-free highway driving could buy GM's Super Cruise or Tesla FSD, so the demand pre-exists and a competitor lost this sale. Test (b) prior alternative: a paid competitor product, not doing-without. Test (c) capability gate: FAILS decisively - BlueCruise has shipped since 2021 and Super Cruise since 2017; nothing here needs a capability that did not exist 24 months ago. Test (d) newly economic: no evidence offered. Within Ford's OWN catalog there is no predecessor SKU, which is why this reads S3-ish on first pass - that is exactly the misclassification METHOD warns about, since the test is industry-level counterfactual, not catalog-level novelty. LOAD-BEARING CAVEAT: Ford framed this as "software," not "AI," on the call; the AI characterisation is the log's, on the grounds that hands-free autonomy is a neural-network product. Discount accordingly. Also note what is NOT disclosed: no absolute dollar of Integrated Services revenue. The only absolute figure Farley gave was forward-looking - "we could absolutely see this business, the Integrated Services, being a half point of margin for the company" - which is PROJECTED, not realized.

XPO Transport & logistics · US C4 — Non-tech · non-engineering

XPO's Q2 2026 release (30 Jul 2026) states "we continued to improve labor productivity above target by implementing new AI capabilities across the network, enhancing efficiency"; on the same day's call CEO Mario Harik credited "our workforce planning technology" with improving "productivity by nearly 2.5 points versus last year, which outperformed our quarterly target of 1.5%", and said AI trailer-loading pilots delivered "load quality improved by more than 40%, while damages were reduced by 50%".

Mechanism
Two distinct systems. (1) Workforce planning: forecasting freight volume by door and shift to schedule dock and P&D labour against it, which is where the 2.5-point productivity number comes from. (2) Trailer loading: cameras photograph freight at each tier inside the trailer, a vision model scores load quality and instructs the dock worker to correct it in real time - fewer damages and more cube per trailer, i.e. fewer linehaul miles for the same freight. Rollout across the full network is planned for H2 2026.
Why this shape
C4 - non-tech sector, non-engineering function (dock and P&D operations, the bulk of an LTL carrier's cost base). The reason this is a cost entry and not a revenue shape: every number is an input-cost or quality metric and no revenue, yield or price is attributed anywhere. ATTRIBUTION DRIFT worth recording, and the main reason confidence is not higher: the press release attributes above-target labour productivity to "new AI capabilities", while the transcript attributes the specific 2.5-point figure to "workforce planning technology" without saying AI. The same quarter, the same company, two primary documents, two different attributions - METHOD trap 4 (many parents) in miniature. Trap 5 also applies: this is productivity above target, not a cost line that fell; XPO's adjusted LTL operating ratio of 79.9% has network investment, pricing and fleet as co-drivers. The 40%/50% trailer-loading figures are PILOT-SITE results, not network results, and should not be read as realized.

Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis - 'AI and Productivity: What Firms Are Saying on Earnings Calls' (~490,000 transcripts, 5,198 US listed firms, 2000-2025) Cross-sector (research) · US C4 — Non-tech · non-engineering⚠ Counter-evidence

St. Louis Fed research published 31 Jul 2026 (Ozkan, Kalyani, Sullivan) classifying productivity-related sentences in ~490,000 earnings call transcripts from 5,198 US listed firms finds that "approximately 95% of AI-related productivity sentences referred to future gains, compared with roughly three-fourths of non-AI-related" sentences, that "95% described productivity as increasing, and the share describing a decrease was negligible", and that AI's share of productivity-related sentences went from near zero pre-ChatGPT to "roughly 15% of productivity sentences" by end-2025.

Mechanism
Two-step text classification over the full transcript corpus: extract productivity-related sentences, then use an open-weight model (Qwen3-3B) to label each for AI relevance, sentiment and temporal framing. Measures what management SAYS about productivity, not what productivity did.
Why this shape
Counter-evidence, tagged to the cost ledger because the subject is productivity claims. PLACEMENT CAVEAT, stated plainly: the corpus is not cut by sector or by function, so C4 is a placement of convenience following the NBER WP 34836 precedent (logged 2026-07-30) rather than a claim that the study is about non-tech non-engineering work. Why it matters more than most entries here: it quantifies the exact failure mode this log was built to detect. A 95%-future-tense rate against a ~75% baseline for non-AI productivity talk means AI productivity discussion is roughly 5x more skewed toward the unrealized than ordinary productivity discussion is, and the near-total absence of decrease-sentences means the corpus contains almost no disconfirming management commentary at all. That is a description of narrative, not of results - and it is consistent with what this log has found by hand across six runs.
