A FactSet Insight review (14 Aug 2026) of seven insurers' Q2 2026 earnings calls finds all seven raised AI unprompted and none isolated its economic effect. The only figure attached to AI at all is Travelers' 0.5-point improvement in the Business Insurance underlying loss ratio, which management said AI and innovation investments 'contributed to' without disclosing AI's discrete share. Chubb's CEO said token costs are 'a fraction, a minor fraction' of the gains and that Chubb tracks the comparison in hard dollars internally, but shared no dollar or basis-point figure. Hamilton called AI 'a productivity and intelligence multiplier' with no productivity, ratio or volume figures attached; American Financial Group cited 'encouraging productivity improvements, particularly in claims' with no figures; AIG, MetLife and Selective attributed no ratio move.

Mechanism
Nothing is being measured here except the disclosure itself. The claimed mechanisms - better risk selection, straight-through claims processing, submission triage - are the same ones this log has recorded from Aviva, Skyward, Ryan Specialty and Baldwin. What is new is a systematic cross-carrier check of whether any of them convert into a disclosed number, run across a full quarter of one sector's calls.
Why this shape
Counter-evidence tagged to S1, which is the shape almost every insurance AI disclosure in this log has taken: an input-metric improvement with no revenue conversion. The finding is that the silence is not carrier-specific but sector-wide. Seven carriers, one quarter, one partial loss-ratio figure that is explicitly not attributed to AI alone - and no carrier put AI next to premium, hit ratio, or new business at all. Note the direction of the residual claim too: even the one number is a loss-ratio (cost/underwriting) figure, not a top-line one, so the sector's own best case for AI is currently a margin story it will not size.

Canadian Imperial Bank of Commerce (CIBC) Banking · Canada S1 — Velocity

On the Q3 2026 earnings call (27 Aug 2026) CEO Harry Culham said CIBC launched AdvisorAssist, 'an AI-enabled platform that helps advisers spend more time with clients. The system automates meeting notes, summaries and follow-up documentation while supporting regulatory compliance, and this reduces administrative time for advisers by up to 50%.' Culham also announced 'CIBC AI 2.0, the first enterprise-wide agentic AI workspace in Canadian banking.' No revenue, net-new-assets, or client-acquisition figure was attached to either.

Mechanism
Advisers in wealth and private banking spend a large fraction of the working week on meeting documentation and compliance write-ups. Automating that returns hours to the client-facing calendar, which is the input to advice sales and asset gathering. The revenue path runs through adviser capacity, not through any change in what CIBC sells.
Why this shape
S1 on the METHOD test: the disclosed figure is an input metric (adviser time), and the process it feeds - adviser client coverage - is revenue-producing. Revenue conversion was conspicuously absent: nothing in the transcript links AI to net new assets, wealth revenue or client acquisition, and the CFO's only AI reference was to 'AI-enabled productivity across our bank.' The same figure has a defensible C4 reading as back-office cost; logged once, as S1, because the disclosure's stated purpose is adviser client capacity rather than expense. Note also that 'up to 50%' is a ceiling, not an average - the honest read is that CIBC has disclosed the best case for one workflow.

TD Bank Group Banking · Canada C4 — Non-tech · non-engineering

On the Q3 2026 earnings call (27 Aug 2026) CEO Raymond Chun said: 'Three quarters into the year, we have essentially hit our fiscal 2026 target of CAD 200 million in value from AI', with C$195 million delivered through three quarters. TD's stated medium-term ambition is C$1 billion of AI value, split as C$500 million of annualized revenue uplift and C$500 million of annualized cost savings. The three named transformation areas are retail end-to-end credit, the software development cycle and contact centres. Group Head Sona Mehta said TD 'fully scaled our first resi agentic AI capability, and it's absolutely delivering faster decisions.'

Mechanism
Document review and decisioning in retail credit, code generation in the software development cycle, and call automation in contact centres. TD Insurance additionally runs AI-powered vehicle damage estimation on claims.
Why this shape
Logged in the cost ledger, and specifically as an instance of METHOD's composite-value trap. 'Value from AI' is a company-defined construct: TD's own medium-term framing splits C$1 billion evenly between revenue uplift and cost savings, which means the realized C$195 million is by construction neither a disclosed cost reduction nor a disclosed revenue gain, and TD did not break it out. Placed in C4 because contact centres and retail credit operations dominate the named use cases, but one of the three - the software development cycle - properly belongs in C3, and TD does not size the cells either. One further tell worth recording: none of these figures appear in TD's own Q3 2026 results release, which mentions AI only as 'investing in front-line talent, AI and innovation to deepen client relationships.' The number lives in oral remarks, not in the filed document.
