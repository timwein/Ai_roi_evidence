Aviva plc Insurance · UK S2 — Share

On the H1 2026 results call (14 Aug 2026) CEO Amanda Blanc said: "U.K. Personal Lines is a great example. We have used AI in our pricing models to deliver over GBP 200 million of run rate benefits here. That is on top of GBP 100 million of claims cost savings previously mentioned." The GBP 200 million figure appears nowhere in the H1 2026 investor presentation, which quantifies AI only as accuracy and time-reduction metrics plus approximately GBP 40 million of claims cost reductions against a stated ambition of more than GBP 50 million.

Mechanism
Machine-learned pricing models in UK personal motor and home: risk selection and demand-elastic rate-setting at the quote, so Aviva wins the risks it prices better than the market and declines or loads the ones it does not. Aviva has said it has used AI and machine learning in pricing "for over a decade," so this is an accumulated run-rate, not a frontier-model effect.
Why this shape
S2, not S3. Test: absent the AI, would this buyer have bought similar cover from somebody? In the UK, motor insurance is compulsory and home insurance near-universal among mortgaged owners - every one of these policies would have been written by some carrier. Better pricing moves the policy from a competitor to Aviva and moves margin within the industry; it creates no new demand and no new insurable population. Zero-sum at industry level. Standing trap 3 applies with force: "run rate benefits" is a company-defined composite that Aviva does not decompose, and it is not a revenue number. The one thing that argues for the revenue ledger over the cost ledger is that Blanc explicitly separates it from the claims cost savings in the same sentence, which places it on the pricing and premium side of the P&L rather than the expense side. If a later disclosure shows the GBP 200 million is predominantly loss-ratio improvement, this entry belongs in C4 and should be moved.

Aviva plc Insurance · UK S1 — Velocity

On the same H1 2026 call Blanc said AI has cut commercial-lines quote generation "from days to hours, which is driving higher conversion," and that in Protection "we have halved the amount of time it takes to review each case in medical underwriting with near perfect accuracy." The written presentation states the underwriting figure as "more than 45% reduction in case summarization time" at "99.99% accuracy." No bound premium, conversion rate or new-business figure is attached to either claim.

Mechanism
Two separate revenue-producing processes. In commercial lines, automated submission ingestion and risk digitisation compress the broker quote turnaround, and in a broker-intermediated market the first acceptable quote back wins a disproportionate share. In Protection, an AI case summariser compresses medical underwriting review, shortening the gap between application and policy issue where applications lapse.
Why this shape
S1: both disclosed numbers are input metrics - cycle time and accuracy - not revenue. Revenue conversion is conspicuously absent, and the absence is specific rather than general: Blanc asserts the quote-time compression is "driving higher conversion" and then gives no conversion figure, in the same speech in which she was willing to quantify a GBP 200 million pricing benefit. Aviva's H1 personal-lines premium growth of roughly 98% is the Direct Line acquisition, not AI, and the company does not claim otherwise. Note also the venue discrepancy on the same metric: "halved" spoken, "more than 45%" written, and "review" spoken versus "case summarization" written - the spoken version is both rounder and broader.

Aviva plc Insurance · UK C4 — Non-tech · non-engineering

Aviva's H1 2026 investor presentation states claims cost reductions of approximately GBP 40 million, "approaching the greater than GBP 50 million ambition," alongside AI claims handlers covering more than 150,000 calls with more than 50% of personal-lines motor call routing automated, and an AI virtual assistant launching in 2026 to handle more than 15 million UK customer interactions annually. In Wealth, agentic AI automating quality assurance "will save 50% of time for our back office teams." On the same day's call the CEO referred instead to "GBP 100 million of claims cost savings previously mentioned."

Mechanism
Claims: automated first-notification-of-loss triage, voice routing and settlement decisioning in UK personal motor. Wealth: agentic quality assurance replacing manual file review in the back office. Non-tech company, non-engineering function, so C4 - the cell that matters.
Why this shape
C4. Two traps apply and both should be recorded. Trap 3, composite metric: "claims cost reductions" is undecomposed and could contain indemnity leakage, fraud detection and handling expense in unknown proportion. More seriously, the spoken and written figures do not agree - GBP 100 million on the call against approximately GBP 40 million against a greater-than-GBP-50-million ambition in the deck, published the same morning. They may cover different scopes or periods; Aviva does not say, and this entry does not assume they are the same number. Trap 5 also applies to the Wealth claim, which is future tense - a projected time saving, not a realized expense reduction, and time saved is not money saved unless headcount or contractor spend actually falls. Aviva discloses roughly GBP 450 million a year of business-as-usual change investment including AI and data, which is the run-cost offset (trap 6) and is not netted against any of these figures.
