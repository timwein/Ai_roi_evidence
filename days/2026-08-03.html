The asymmetry is the finding. CCC will tell you AI is 11% of its revenue. The "top five insurers" deploying that same AI are named only as "top five insurers", and have disclosed nothing. Every quantified AI revenue number available this week sits one layer up the value chain from the company actually using the AI.

Worth naming a second-order pattern across the three cost entries, because it cuts against the loudest version of the cost thesis: all three companies declined to bank a revenue consequence, and two of them said so explicitly. WTW routes the benefit through capacity redeployment. Baldwin says the benefit is "embedded in the numbers". This is METHOD trap 2 showing up not as one CEO's caution but as a sector convention in insurance services.

And the magnitudes deserve stating plainly. Baldwin's disclosure is the honest one and it is about 15 basis points of revenue. WTW's is $400m but costs $625m to get and does not arrive until 2028. Neither is a margin event yet.

CCC Intelligent Solutions Software - insurance claims · US S2 — Share

On the Q2 2026 call (30 Jul 2026) CCC disclosed "over $120 million of annualized revenue from AI-based solutions that are growing at nearly 50% year-over-year", representing "approximately 11% of total revenue in the second quarter and growing approximately 45% year-over-year", and said "revenue from AI-based solutions contributed 4 points of growth", driven by APD, subrogation and EvolutionIQ. Named deployments: "a top five insurer became its largest carrier to date to adopt the AI-powered subrogation solution", and "two top five insurers expanded their use of AI-enabled claims workflows by deploying the First Look solution to identify total losses earlier".

Mechanism
Computer-vision damage appraisal and AI claims workflow sold as add-on modules into CCC's existing installed base of auto insurers and collision repairers - subrogation demand packaging, early total-loss identification, and medical-claim synthesis via the acquired EvolutionIQ technology.
Why this shape
Appendix: the subject is a software vendor, out of scope for the main revenue log - CCC's AI revenue is its own sales, not adoption ROI at the deploying carriers. Shape S2, not S3, and the distinction is the point: these are add-on modules sold into an installed base that already buys CCC's claims platform, and the work displaced was either a competitor's tool or the carrier's own unpaid manual staff time. Test (a) fails - absent CCC's AI the carriers would still be processing subrogation and total losses, and paying somebody to do it. Test (c) is arguable for the vision models but not established by the source. The genuinely useful reading is the asymmetry: the quantified AI revenue sits at the vendor, and the carriers deploying it - all named only as 'top five insurers' - have disclosed no corresponding revenue or loss-ratio number of their own. That is the shape of this entire log in one disclosure.

On the Q2 2026 call (30 Jul 2026) WTW launched programme 'Propel', committing to spend "approximately $625 million in cash" plus "approximately $25 million in non-cash charges to produce approximately $400 million in run-rate savings" by the end of 2028, from "embedding AI and automation across our business" (CEO Carl Hess). CFO Andrew Krasner tied it to "2028 adjusted operating margin targets of 30% at the enterprise, 35% in HWC, and 30% in R&B". No revenue uplift was quantified: asked where growth comes from, Krasner said "the growth comes from how we redeploy that capacity that gets generated over time."

Mechanism
Broking and HWC workflow automation - the named live tools are Navigator and Neuron, described as "not pilots, they're live and in daily use" - plus process automation across a labour-heavy advisory cost base. The saving is headcount and process cost in client-facing and support functions, not engineering.
Why this shape
C4: non-tech (insurance broking and HR/benefits advisory), non-engineering function - the cost base being attacked is broking and consulting labour. Not a revenue shape at any point: WTW quantified the spend, the saving and the margin target, and explicitly declined to quantify a revenue uplift, routing it instead through capacity redeployment. That is METHOD trap 2 in partial form - management banks the cost saving in guidance but leaves the revenue leg entirely qualitative. Note also the ratio: $625m of cash out to get $400m of annual run-rate saving is a ~1.6x payback-year gross, i.e. the run-cost offset is being paid up front and disclosed, which is rarer and better than most cost claims in this ledger.

The Baldwin Insurance Group Insurance distribution · US C4 — Non-tech · non-engineering

On the Q2 2026 call (30 Jul 2026) Baldwin disclosed "47 thousand tasks completed via AI over the past 17 weeks, maintaining a quality rate exceeding 99%" across "27 processes already optimized", and gave one fully specified realized saving: direct bill reconciliation "reduced run rate costs from $3 million to $1 million a year", with "internal labor cost on the process from $1.2 million to roughly $400 thousand", while reconciliation quality improved "from roughly 90% in month to a sustained 98%". Management said the model is "running at production scale, not pilot scale" and that AI benefits are embedded in the numbers via efficiency; no revenue figure was attributed to AI.

Mechanism
Agentic automation of insurance-brokerage back office - matching carrier direct-bill statements to commission records, plus 26 other service-line processes across commercial and benefits.
Why this shape
C4: non-tech (insurance distribution), non-engineering function - brokerage operations labour. This is the most methodologically clean cost entry in the ledger to date because it is realized rather than projected, names the specific process, and gives a before-and-after on a single line item rather than a composite. It is also the entry that shows the size problem most clearly: a $2m annual run-rate reduction at a company with roughly $1.4bn of annual revenue is about 15 basis points. Rule 1 applies to the headline: '47,000 tasks' is an adoption metric and says nothing about cost.
