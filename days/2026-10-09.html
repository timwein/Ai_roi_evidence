The Very Group (Very, Littlewoods) Retail / E-commerce · UK S2 — Share

In a company press release dated 5 Oct 2026, The Very Group - annual revenue "over GBP 2bn", `4.2m` customers - reported the first month of an AI personalisation deployment with vendor Made With Intent. Digital director Sarah Hanna: "we've already seen promising results including revenue per customer increasing by a fifth in one of our agentic strategies." A conversion uplift was claimed but not quantified. No base, no control group, no comparison period, no absolute revenue and no customer count for the measured cohort were disclosed, and the `20%` applies to one agentic strategy rather than the site.

Mechanism
Made With Intent's decision engine reads "over 800 behavioural signals" to predict shopper intent in real time and decides what each visitor sees and when, including discount offers targeted to individual shopping patterns and prompts placed through the journey. An optimisation agent tests experiences across hundreds of intent combinations and applies the winners to later visits.
Why this shape
S2: general-merchandise and apparel sold to existing Very and Littlewoods customers. Counterfactual purchase - yes, these shoppers would have bought comparable goods, from Very or from a competitor, so a rival marketplace loses the sale; zero-sum at industry level and GDP-neutral. Capability gate fails for S3: real-time behavioural scoring and offer targeting is 2019-era ML in newer language, whatever the "agentic" label. Note the mechanism includes targeted discounting, so revenue per customer can rise while gross margin falls - the company disclosed no margin figure.

The retrieved Reuters report does not state the sample size, the fielding dates or the full country list.

Mechanism

Why this shape
Counter-evidence tagged to S2 - the shape it contradicts - because it is a direct measurement of how rarely AI benefit shows up as attributed revenue rather than cost. Levi Strauss & Co. Retail / Apparel · US S1 — Velocity

On the Q3 FY2026 earnings call (7 Oct 2026) President and CEO Michelle Gass gave the first result from the AI shopping assistant launched on Levi.com in the US: "While still early, we're seeing fans who engage with the tool adding to bag at roughly three times our average rate." The revenue line next to it is unattributed: Gass said only that "Our e-commerce channel delivered another quarter of double-digit growth" (one transcript rendering puts e-commerce at `10%` organic), credited jointly to richer digital storytelling and the assistant launch. Group net revenues were `$1.61bn`, up `4%` reported and `5%` organic; DTC rose `2%` with flat comparable sales. No share of e-commerce growth, and no dollar of revenue, was attributed to the assistant.

Mechanism
A conversational assistant on Levi.com gives styling advice, helps shoppers discover styles and builds outfits, with the intended effect of moving browsers into baskets. The disclosed metric is add-to-bag rate for the cohort that engaged with the tool, measured against the site average.
Why this shape
S1 because the disclosed number is add-to-bag rate - a funnel conversion input - not revenue. Revenue conversion is conspicuously absent rather than merely unstated: the company had a revenue number for the same channel in the same breath (double-digit e-commerce growth) and declined to attribute any part of it to the tool, instead bracketing it with "enhanced digital storytelling". The `3x` figure is also a self-selected cohort - shoppers who choose to engage a styling assistant are higher-intent before the assistant touches them - so it measures neither lift nor incrementality. Not S2: no revenue is attributed. Not S3: jeans sold on a brand's own site to its existing customers, with every prior alternative intact.

Evident AI Index for Banks - 2026 edition preview (Bank of America, Lloyds Banking Group, JPMorganChase, Commonwealth Bank of Australia named) Banking (research) · Global C4 — Non-tech · non-engineering⚠ Counter-evidence

Evident Insights' banking brief of 1 Oct 2026 normalises the bank AI-value disclosures and the result is small. Bank of America's figure is "about 0.7% of revenue" and Lloyds "estimated it would generate some $133 million in 2026 - roughly 0.5% of its 2025 revenue". Evident states that Bank of America is "the 13th bank in the Evident AI Index for Banks to report realized or projected returns from its AI portfolio", up from eight a year earlier, that it now runs `130` Gen AI use cases in production against `114` in July, and that it is "one of the only banks to publicly say it's in the black on AI". Evident records that the returns "combine revenue gains and cost savings" and gives no split. For context it notes Jamie Dimon said a year ago that JPMorganChase had broken even on AI, and that CommBank said it would reach breakeven if it hit its AI targets next year.

Mechanism
Not a deployment. Evident tracks which of the banks in its index have put any number on AI returns, and normalises those numbers against group revenue. The mechanism being measured is disclosure behaviour, which is the only cross-sectional denominator currently available for bank AI claims.
Why this shape
C4 - non-tech sector, non-engineering function - because the thing being normalised is a company-defined composite "value" metric with cost inside it, which METHOD trap 3 puts in the cost ledger, not the revenue shapes. Counter-evidence: the two best-disclosed bank AI programmes in the world amount to `0.5%` and `0.7%` of revenue, the composite is never split into revenue and cost, and only `13` of the index's banks disclose any return at all. Not double-logged: the BofA `$400m`/`$800m` statement (2026-09-15, C4) and the Lloyds `GBP 100m`-plus statement (2026-08-12, C4) are already here; this entry is Evident's distinct dated claim - the percentage-of-revenue normalisation and the 13-versus-8 disclosure count - not a restatement of either.
