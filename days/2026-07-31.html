Tempus AI Healthcare / precision diagnostics · US S1 — Velocity⚠ Counter-evidence

On the Q2 2026 call (30 Jul 2026) CFO Jim Rogers disclosed: "The algorithm attach rate in Q2 is 45%, a slight uptick from the 40% that we had quoted in Q1" - and then removed the revenue reading himself: "Many of those algorithms remain not being paid, and so there's no impact on revenue from the number of algorithms." Q2 revenue $382.5m (+22% YoY); Diagnostics $289.3m (+20%); Data and Apps $93.2m (+28%). The company's own guided uplift - "an estimated $200 uplift in ASP, which equates to approximately $85 million on an annual basis beginning in 2027" and ~$400m by 2028 - is tied to FDA approval of tumor-only xT CDx and unified ADLT pricing, i.e. to a reimbursement event, not to the algorithms.

Mechanism
AI algorithms (multi-modal models scoring pathology, imaging and molecular data) are ordered alongside a sequencing test and delivered in the report. Attach rate is the share of tests that carry at least one algorithm. The intended revenue path is per-algorithm reimbursement; that path is largely not yet open, so consumption rises while the associated revenue stays at zero.
Why this shape
S1 - the disclosed number is an input metric (attach rate), not revenue. The S1 standing caveat is satisfied in the strongest possible way: the company did not merely omit the revenue conversion, the CFO volunteered that it is zero. Logged as counter-evidence against S3: this is a frontier-capability AI product, deployed at commercial scale, consumed by clinicians on 45% of tests, generating no incremental revenue - the capability gate is passed and the newly-economic gate is not. Scope is appendix: Tempus is an AI-native diagnostics company, out of scope as a revenue-log subject; it is retained as a comparator because entrants quantify what incumbents will not, and because it is the cleanest available separation of AI capability from AI monetisation.

Hippo Holdings Insurance · US C4 — Non-tech · non-engineering

On the Q2 2026 call (30 Jul 2026) CEO Rick McCathron said two named AI agents went live in the quarter - "Hannah, our AI service agent, and Clara, our AI First Notice of Loss agent, are both live this quarter" - and that "together they're a big part of why we can grow the top line without growing overhead at the same pace." CFO Guy Zeltser: "Our continued focus on operating leverage through AI enables us to grow our business while keeping fixed expense largely flat." Q2 revenue $144.7m; net expense ratio 45.4% (-8pp YoY); fixed expense ratio 29%, down 39 points since the start of 2024.

Mechanism
Two customer-facing LLM agents in the non-engineering cost base: Hannah handles inbound policy service contacts, Clara takes first notice of loss. Both sit where headcount would otherwise scale with policy count, so the claimed effect is a flat fixed-expense base under a growing book rather than an expense line that falls.
Why this shape
C4 - non-tech sector (a licensed home insurance carrier; revenue is premium and commission, not software) and a non-engineering function (service and claims intake). Cost ledger, not a revenue shape: management framed this as operating leverage, and no revenue is attributed to either agent. Trap 5 applies - this is cost avoidance (headcount not added), not a realized reduction, and management said so in those words. Trap 1 also present and deliberately not logged as a saving: the same call disclosed Devin, Cognition's AI software engineer, rolled out to "nearly a third of our roughly 500 employees" - an adoption metric with no outcome attached, which is why it is noted here rather than entered as a C3. The 39-point fixed-expense-ratio decline since early 2024 predates both agents and is not AI-attributable; only the Q2 flat-fixed-expense claim is.
