Elixirr International plc Professional services (management consulting) · UK S2 — Share

In its H1 2026 interim results for the half-year ended 30 Jun 2026, reported 22 Sep 2026, Elixirr disclosed AI revenue of `GBP 8.1m`, up `185%` from `GBP 2.8m` in H1 2025, against total revenue of `GBP 89.0m`, up `25%` from `GBP 71.4m` - so AI is roughly `9.1%` of group revenue. CEO Stephen Newton: "We have spent the last six years building the consulting, AI, data and technology capabilities to benefit from the AI shift" and "The conversation around AI is moving quickly from understanding what the technology can do to figuring out how to put it to work." The company frames the opportunity as a `USD 200bn` agentic AI services market over five years. No client-side ROI number is disclosed anywhere in the release: this is Elixirr's revenue from selling AI work, not a client's return on buying it.

Mechanism
Three revenue streams inside the AI line: paid assessments that identify use cases and produce implementation roadmaps; building what Elixirr calls "AI-native businesses from the ground up" for clients; and AI-enabled delivery embedded inside ordinary consulting engagements. Elixirr OS, a proprietary agentic platform launched in H1 2026, gives consultants and agents shared client and firm context; the firm cites over 20 client implementations and one internal example of a 60-page report and three financial models delivered in four weeks against a seven-to-eight-week estimate.
Why this shape
Appendix, because the subject is out of scope under METHOD: Elixirr is a consultancy selling AI services, so this measures shovel sales, not adoption ROI. Logged S2 rather than S3-CANDIDATE because the default reading of consulting fees is displacement, not new demand. Test (a): absent the AI, these clients were buying transformation consulting from somebody - Elixirr competes directly with the Big Four and the systems integrators for the same budget line. Test (b): the prior alternative was a paid engagement, not nothing. The only part with an S3 reading is the "AI-native businesses from the ground up" work, which Elixirr does not size separately and does not tie to any client that would otherwise have gone unserved. The firm also does not say how much of the `GBP 8.1m` is net-new logos versus AI work sold into existing accounts, which is the split that would decide the shape.

Barclays PLC Banking · UK C4 — Non-tech · non-engineering

At the BofA Securities 31st Annual Financials CEO Conference on 22 Sep 2026, CFO Anna Cross said Barclays' contact-centre AI "has cut `6,000` hours" and that AI tools "have reduced fraud-related inbound calls by about `20%`". The rendering paraphrases her deployment posture as preferring standard machine learning where suitable and using agentic tools selectively, and records roughly `80%` of data moved onto the enterprise platform against a `100%` target by end-2028. In the same appearance Barclays committed a further `GBP 300m` of technology and simplification spend in H2 2026, running at about `GBP 250m` a quarter. No cost saving in currency, no headcount and no revenue were attributed to AI.

Mechanism
AI handling routine servicing contacts in the contact centre, plus AI in fraud detection and customer communication that removes the inbound call altogether rather than handling it faster - the second is the more interesting mechanism because it suppresses demand for the contact rather than automating the response. Data consolidation onto a single enterprise platform is the stated enabling layer.
Why this shape
C4 - non-tech sector, non-engineering function, the cell METHOD weights most heavily, but a weak instance of it. "`6,000` hours" carries no denominator, no period and no currency equivalent, so it cannot be sized against any cost base; against a contact-centre workforce of Barclays' scale it may be immaterial. The `20%` fraud-call reduction is the better number because its denominator is at least implicit. Trap 5 applies squarely: neither figure is stated as a realized reduction in an expense line. Trap 6 is visible in the same breath - `GBP 300m` of *additional* technology spend disclosed alongside the saving is the run-cost offset sitting in plain view.
