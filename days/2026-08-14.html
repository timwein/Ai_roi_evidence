RadNet Healthcare (diagnostic imaging) · US S3 candidate (unproven)

On the Q2 2026 call (10 Aug 2026) CFO Mark Stolper disclosed that "Revenue from the EBCD program increased 16%" year over year. EBCD - Enhanced Breast Cancer Detection - is an AI second read on screening mammography that no payer reimburses and that RadNet sells directly to the patient as a self-pay add-on. It sits inside a Digital Health quarter of $32.4m (+56.5%), of which AI revenue was $16.1m, up 136%.

Mechanism
An algorithm reads the screening mammogram alongside the radiologist. Because no US payer covers a second read, RadNet prices it to the patient at the point of appointment. The revenue is out-of-pocket consumer spend on a diagnostic enhancement that did not previously exist as a purchasable item, booked through the Digital Health segment.
Why this shape
Four tests run. (a) Counterfactual purchase - PASSES: the covered screening mammogram happens either way; the EBCD fee is an additional transaction that does not exist without the algorithm. (b) Prior alternative - PASSES: there was no competing product and no unpaid workaround; routine human double-reading is not done in the US, so patients simply went without. (c) Capability gate - FAILS: mammography CAD on deep learning long predates a 24-month window and RadNet has sold EBCD since 2021-22. This is new TAM opened by pre-frontier ML, not by the current capability jump. (d) Newly economic - PASSES: an algorithmic second read at a self-pay price point supports a service that human double-reading never could. Held at S3-CANDIDATE rather than promoted, for one reason: RadNet has never disclosed EBCD in dollars, so a 16% growth rate sits on an unknown base and the absolute size of the new demand cannot be checked. Note also that the $16.1m AI revenue line is mostly RadNet-as-vendor - DeepHealth software sold to third-party hospitals - which is shovels, not deployment ROI, and is excluded from this entry.

Aviva plc Insurance · UK S1 — Velocity

Aviva's H1 2026 analyst presentation (14 Aug 2026, slide 33) discloses a ">45% Reduction in case summarisation time" in medical underwriting from AI medical-report summarisation running at "99.99% accuracy, accepted by re-insurers", and CEO Amanda Blanc told the results call Aviva is "using AI in commercial lines to reduce the time it takes to generate quotes from days to hours."

Mechanism
Medical underwriting is the throughput gate on protection and health policy issuance; commercial-lines quote generation is the gate on quote-to-bind. Compressing both raises the number of revenue-producing decisions the same underwriting force can complete, and the re-insurer acceptance of the AI summaries is what makes the faster output bindable rather than merely faster.
Why this shape
S1: every disclosed number is an input metric - time, accuracy, automation rate - not revenue. Revenue conversion is conspicuously absent. Aviva reported Health in-force premiums up 5% and Health operating profit up 28% to GBP 37m and attributed neither to AI, while cutting expected full-year Health operating profit to around GBP 90m against a GBP 100m target. Aviva's own slide 19 is unusually candid about why no revenue number exists: AI benefits are deployed three ways - "Reinvest in next opportunity", "Realise in bottom line", "Factor into trading decisions" - so management states on the record that it does not bank the gain as one thing.

Heartflow Healthcare (medical diagnostics) · US S3 candidate (unproven)

In its Q2 2026 results (8-K Ex 99.1, 13 Aug 2026) Heartflow reported revenue of $64.1m, up 48% year over year (US $59.6m, +51%), and raised full-year 2026 guidance to "$246 million to $250 million (approximately 40% to 42% growth year-over-year), compared to previous guidance of $228 million to $232 million". The release names "an increase in total U.S. Plaque revenue case volume" as a driver of both the revenue growth and the record gross margin, and CEO John Farquhar called Plaque "a meaningful second growth engine".

Mechanism
Heartflow Plaque Analysis is an AI quantification of non-calcified coronary plaque from a coronary CT angiogram - a measurement not routinely produced before, and separately billable in the US from 2026 under a Category I CPT code. It is sold alongside the existing FFRCT product into the same installed base of more than 1,800 institutions, so the incremental sale requires no new customer.
Why this shape
Subject is out of scope for the main log and routed to the appendix under METHOD's scope-tension rule: Heartflow describes itself as "the leader in AI technology for diagnosing coronary artery disease" and the product is the AI, so it is an AI-native, not an incumbent deploying AI. S3 tests: (a) counterfactual purchase - PARTIAL: plaque burden was previously either not assessed or approximated by a calcium score, so some of this spend is substitution rather than new demand. (b) Prior alternative - PARTIAL: a weaker one existed (calcium scoring, invasive angiography). (c) Capability gate - FAILS: the Plaque product has been FDA-cleared since 2022; what changed in 2026 was reimbursement, not capability. (d) Newly economic - PASSES: a Category I code made a previously unpaid analysis billable. Logged as a candidate and as the entrant-side comparator to RadNet's in-scope EBCD entry the same day.

Aviva plc Insurance · UK C4 — Non-tech · non-engineering

On the H1 2026 results call (14 Aug 2026) CEO Amanda Blanc said Aviva has "used AI in our pricing models to deliver over GBP 200 million of run rate benefits" in UK Personal Lines, "on top of GBP 100 million of claims cost savings". The published presentation (slide 32) attributes the GBP 100m to ">250 ML models in PL pricing & claims", and slide 33 adds "c.100 Wealth FTE capacity created", ">50% Personal Lines Motor call routing automated" and "50% Time saved" on agentic quality assurance.

Mechanism
More than 250 machine-learning models running in UK Personal Lines pricing and claims. On the claims side the benefit is indemnity cost - better triage, fraud detection and settlement accuracy. On the pricing side it is risk selection and rate adequacy, which lands in the loss ratio rather than in premium.
Why this shape
C4 - non-tech, non-engineering, the cell that matters. Logged in the cost ledger rather than as revenue because that is where the number sits: "run rate benefits" from pricing models is METHOD's standing trap 3, an undefined composite that can contain loss-ratio improvement, rate adequacy and conversion in unknown proportions, and Aviva does not decompose it. Slide 19 confirms the construct is deliberately non-specific: benefits are split between reinvestment, the bottom line and trading decisions. Trap 6 also appears on the record - CFO Charlotte Jones said the company monitors Claude token usage and restricts those costs like any other business expense, which is the run-cost offset acknowledged rather than dodged. On a single-year run-rate basis this is the largest AI-attributed benefit figure a non-tech incumbent has put on the record anywhere in this log.
