Walmart Retail · US S1 — Velocity

On the Q2 FY2027 call (20 Aug 2026) CEO John Furner said: "The number of customers using Sparky is up 70% from last year, and the customers and members who use Sparky for shopping spend 40% more per order than others who do not." The per-order differential is up from the 35% Walmart disclosed on the Q4 FY2026 call (19 Feb 2026). Global eCommerce grew 23% and global advertising 38% in the quarter; neither was attributed to AI and no incremental sales dollars were sized.

Mechanism
Sparky, a conversational assistant inside the Walmart app, answers open-ended shopping intents (Furner's example: a weekly high-protein meal plan) and returns recipes, meal kits and a one-click basket of ingredients, plus recognition of items the shopper already bought. The revenue path is cross-category basket building - the assistant converts a single-item errand into a multi-category order.
Why this shape
S1, not S2, and the distinction follows the Amazon precedent logged 2026-08-12: a cohort spend differential is an input/behavioural metric, not attributed incremental revenue. Nobody has established that the same shopper spends more because of Sparky; the disclosed statistic is equally consistent with high-frequency, high-basket shoppers being the ones who try an assistant first. Revenue conversion is conspicuously absent rather than merely unstated - Walmart sized eCommerce (+23%) and advertising (+38%) growth on the same call and attributed neither to AI. Note the convergence: Walmart's cohort statistic (40% more per order) is now numerically identical to Amazon's Alexa for Shopping figure from three weeks earlier. Two of the largest retailers on earth have settled on the same non-causal disclosure format.

Lowe's Companies Retail · US S1 — Velocity

On the Q2 2026 call (19 Aug 2026) Lowe's disclosed that "customers who engage with Mylow while browsing online convert at three times the rate of those who don't", and that Mylow has fielded approximately 25 million customer and associate questions since launch. No revenue, basket or ticket figure was attributed to Mylow, and the quarter's positive comparable sales were not attributed to AI.

Mechanism
Mylow is a generative assistant on lowes.com answering home-improvement project questions in session; the same engine powers a Mylow Companion application for store associates. The claimed revenue path is intent resolution - a shopper who gets a project question answered mid-session completes the purchase instead of abandoning.
Why this shape
S1: conversion rate is an input metric, not revenue. Revenue conversion conspicuously absent - Lowe's put no dollars, ticket or basket figure against Mylow on the same call where it reported comps and raised its store target. Selection bias is severe and unaddressed in the specific direction that inflates the number: a shopper who stops to ask a project question is deeper in purchase intent than the average browser, so a 3x conversion gap is the expected result even if Mylow were inert. Not S2 because no incremental revenue is attributed; not S3 because these buyers were already buying home-improvement product from Lowe's or a competitor.

Albertsons Companies Grocery · US S1 — Velocity

In a Wall Street Journal interview published 17 Aug 2026, Albertsons SVP of Digital Shopping Experiences Jill Pavlovich said average order value rises 10% when customers use the company's conversational search and 26% when they use its more comprehensive assistants that match recipes and ingredients to dietary preferences. Follow-up on the entry logged 2026-07-26: the 10% figure is the same one CEO Susan Morris put on the record on 7 Jan 2026 and did NOT repeat on the 23 Jul Q1 FY2026 call. It has reappeared - in a press interview rather than an SEC-exposed venue - accompanied by a higher second figure.

Mechanism
Conversational search plus a fuller meal-planning assistant that resolves a dietary constraint into a recipe and then into an ingredient list. Pavlovich's stated mechanism is category breadth: "All of a sudden they're shopping across categories, across items, not spearfishing for that single item that they need".
Why this shape
S1 by the same test applied to Amazon (2026-08-12) and Walmart today: an average-order-value differential between users and non-users is a cohort/behavioural input metric, not attributed incremental revenue. The 26% figure is the more interesting one and the weaker one - it describes the deepest-engagement cohort, which is also the most self-selected. What makes this entry worth logging is not the number but the venue. The claim vanished from the July earnings call and returned in a newspaper interview eight weeks later. Partial reverse-falsification of the 2026-07-26 entry, whose falsifier asked whether Albertsons would re-disclose a revenue-attributed AI metric at or above 10% - it has, but not where securities liability attaches.

CGTrader Digital marketplace (3D assets) · LT S3 — New TAM⚠ Counter-evidence

Marketplace transaction data covering June 2025 - May 2026, reported by Fortune on 20 Aug 2026: AI-generated models are now roughly 1 in 6 newly uploaded assets on CGTrader but account for "$1 out of every $90 in generated revenue" (~1.1%) and 2.6% of sales. Only 5% of CGTrader customers who tried an AI-generated asset found it worked well, against 20% who found it inadequate. Alongside: Pew reports 52% of US adults are "more concerned than excited" about greater AI use, up from 38% in 2022.

Mechanism
Generative models collapsed the cost of producing a 3D asset, so supply expanded roughly six-fold as a share of new uploads. The S3 hypothesis this tests is the newly-economic one: if AI-collapsed production cost opens demand that previously could not be served, revenue share should track supply share. It does not - a ~17% share of new supply converts to ~1-3% of transactions.
Why this shape
Counter-evidence tagged to S3, and specifically to test (d), newly economic. This is the cleanest natural experiment retrieved to date on whether an AI-driven cost collapse creates demand: the cost curve moved hard, supply followed, and buyers did not. It contradicts the standing assumption that cheaper production is sufficient for new TAM - on this marketplace it produced catalogue inflation, not a new market. Appendix scope because CGTrader is an online software marketplace and out of scope as a subject in the main revenue log; the finding is about buyer behaviour, not about CGTrader's own AI deployment. Two figures are given for the same phenomenon (1.1% of revenue, 2.6% of sales); read them as revenue share and unit share respectively - Fortune does not reconcile them and neither should this entry.
