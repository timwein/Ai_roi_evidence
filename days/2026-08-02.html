Wyndham Hotels & Resorts Hospitality · US S2 — Share

On the Q2 2026 call (23 Jul 2026) CEO Geoff Ballotti said "roughly 60% of travel searches by our guests are occurring within an LLM" and disclosed "a 20% higher conversion on our brand.com sites when the guest connects to us from an LLM." Wyndham Connect, the AI guest-engagement platform, is in over 5,000 hotels handling "260,000 guest interactions via this AI tool per day" and generates "$100,000 or more in increased revenues" for engaged franchisees; Wyndham AI Concierge, live in 1,500 hotels, delivers "up to ... 500 basis points of direct contribution" and a "15% increase in ADR, if you're booking it autonomously versus on the phone."

Mechanism
Two distinct paths. (1) Demand capture: as guest search migrates into LLMs, Wyndham converts LLM-originated traffic to brand.com at a 20% higher rate than other sources - it is winning the direct booking rather than losing the guest to an OTA. (2) Autonomous booking: the AI Concierge takes voice/SMS reservations end to end at the property, and a room booked autonomously prices 15% above the same booking taken on the phone, because the agent upsells and does not drop calls. The per-franchisee revenue figure is Wyndham's attribution for the most engaged properties, not a rolled-up corporate number.
Why this shape
S2, not S3. Test: absent the AI, would this guest have booked a hotel room? Almost certainly yes - from an OTA, from a competing brand, or from the same property over the phone at a lower ADR. The mechanism is channel capture and price realisation on demand that already existed. Who lost the sale: OTAs (Booking, Expedia), competing economy/midscale brands, and Wyndham's own lower-yielding phone channel. Zero-sum at industry level and GDP-neutral - a guest who was going to sleep somewhere sleeps somewhere slightly more expensive. The 60%-of-searches-in-an-LLM figure is a channel-shift statistic, not a demand-creation one, and should not be read as new TAM.

Ryan Specialty Holdings Insurance (specialty distribution / underwriting managers) · US S1 — Velocity

On the Q2 2026 call (30 Jul 2026) Miles Wuller, CEO of Underwriting Managers, said "the average RSUM property employee ... achieved 11% more quotes per head in the last 12 months than the prior year", attributing it to "our investments in automated data extraction, data structuring, enrichment, and rating pre-population coming to life." CEO Tim Turner said the "reinsurance FAC workbench now turns a submission into a priced decision-ready file in minutes, not days", that the firm has "started rolling out a proprietary engine for deploying AI around the firm", and that the underwriting-managers business is "auditing five times as many files as we did last year."

Mechanism
Submission intake is the bottleneck in specialty underwriting: a broker sends an unstructured schedule of values, and a human keys it into a rating engine. Automated extraction, structuring, enrichment and rating pre-population removes that step, so the same headcount quotes more accounts. Capacity, not price - Wuller's framing is explicitly that efficiency is a countervailing force against a 25-35% decline in catastrophe pricing.
Why this shape
S1: every disclosed number is an input metric - quotes per head, cycle time, files audited. Revenue conversion was conspicuously NOT disclosed. Management said the unlocked capacity "is being directed back into what matters most, winning new business" and that "high retention and new-business wins limited the decline in property revenue", but attached no AI-attributed revenue number to either. More quotes is not more bound premium: in a softening property market a higher quote count can simply mean more declined quotes at inadequate rates. Note also the honesty of the attribution - Wuller conceded the gain "certainly includes hustle", and the named mechanisms (data extraction, rating pre-population) are automation more than frontier AI, so the capability gate is weak even though the firm frames it inside its AI rollout.

BIS Working Paper 1325 - 'AI adoption, productivity and employment: evidence from European firms' (12,000+ non-financial EU/US firms) Cross-sector (research) · EU / US C4 — Non-tech · non-engineering

Instrumental-variables study of matched EIBIS-ORBIS data on "more than 12,000 non-financial firms in the European Union (EU) and United States (US)", instrumenting EU firms' AI adoption with the adoption rates of matched US peers: "AI adoption increases the level of labor productivity by 4%." The authors find "no adverse effects on firm-level employment", attribute the gain to capital deepening, and find the benefit concentrated in medium and large firms.

Mechanism
Not a deployment finding - a causal estimate of the effect size the rest of this ledger is trying to measure one company at a time. Uses matched US peer adoption as an instrument to break the endogeneity between being a good firm and adopting AI, which is the flaw in every cross-sectional adoption-vs-performance correlation.
Why this shape
C4: sample is non-financial firms, effect measured at the whole-firm level rather than in an engineering function. Logged as cost/productivity, not revenue - the outcome is labor productivity, and the paper explicitly attributes it to capital deepening rather than to new demand, so nothing here supports S1-S3. Its value is as a magnitude check: 4% is one to two orders of magnitude below the 30-40% self-reported gains sitting elsewhere in this ledger (Amex, BNY), and it is the only causally identified estimate in the ledger. Backlog entry, not fresh news - published 23 Jan 2026 and logged now because the C ledger otherwise contains only surveys and text-mining and was missing its best-identified datapoint. Age is stated so nobody reads it as a new disclosure.

Falsifier
The instrument assumes US peer adoption affects EU firm outcomes only through EU adoption; if AI adoption by US peers hits EU firms through competition or supply chains, the estimate is biased. Falsified if later vintages of EIBIS with post-2024 frontier-model adoption fail to reproduce a positive productivity effect, or if the 4% shrinks toward zero once the pre-generative-AI adopters are dropped from the sample.
Confidence
medium — Central-bank working paper with a plausible identification strategy and a large sample, but not peer reviewed, and the adoption measure is survey-reported and largely pre-dates frontier generative models - so 4% is likely an estimate of the older ML wave, not of what firms are buying in 2026.
