The pattern this week is which metric survives. Inside one Etsy document the flattering number got a figure and a repeat on the call; the unflattering one lost its figure and gained an adjective. Booking did the opposite - it kept quantifying its weakest channel at under 1% while adding three new disclosures. Selective quantification is now the most reliable tell in this log, and it is cheap to audit: note the number, wait a quarter, see if it is still there.

The second pattern is the size of honest numbers. Where a company volunteers a precise AI revenue contribution rather than a multiple or a growth rate, the figure keeps coming in small - NIQ at 0.1 point of 2026 growth, Etsy at 2 points of annualized GMS growth off a 100m-listing matching problem. The large AI numbers in this log remain cost-side or input-side, and the ECB's 761-call read says that is what managements are mostly claiming in the first place.

Etsy Retail / e-commerce marketplace · US S2 — Share

The cleanest revenue-attributed AI number retrieved this run, and it was sitting unlogged for 16 days. Etsy's Q3 2026 shareholder letter (28 Oct 2026) states: "by the third quarter, we estimate that personalization improvements alone contributed approximately two percentage points to our annualized year-over-year GMS growth rate." CEO Kruti Patel Goyal repeated it in prepared remarks on the call the same day and CFO Lanny Baker characterised it as "a couple of points of growth". Q3 GMS was `$2,646,888` thousand, up `8.8%`, on revenue of `$692,469` thousand, up `10.9%`.

Mechanism
AI and machine-learning ranking applied to a 100m-plus listing catalogue where the constraint is matching, not supply. The letter names three concrete changes in the quarter: extending personalization to signed-out users, expanding buyer profiles that now power "personalized feeds, recommendations, and owned marketing for four out of five active buyers", and re-weighting the Search objective away from next-click toward "signals like value and the expected post-purchase experience".
Why this shape
S2, not S3, on all four tests. (a) Counterfactual purchase: these are existing and returning buyers already in-market for handmade, vintage and personalised goods. (b) Prior alternative: absent Etsy's ranking they buy comparable inventory on Amazon Handmade, eBay or direct from the same sellers' own storefronts - a competitor loses the sale, so this is a share transfer and GDP-neutral at industry level. (c) Capability gate fails outright: recommender personalization and learned ranking at this level were production technology well before 2024, and nothing in the letter requires a frontier capability. (d) Not a newly economic segment. What makes it worth logging is evidentiary rather than economic: a non-tech incumbent putting a specific percentage-point figure on AI's contribution to its own top line is rare in this log, and most of the 54 S2 entries here have no number this clean. The attribution is nevertheless Etsy's own estimate with no disclosed methodology.

NIQ Global Intelligence plc Information services / consumer measurement · US / UK S2 — Share

The most precisely sized AI revenue expectation retrieved this run, and the number is `10` basis points. On the Q3 2026 call (12 Nov 2026) Executive Chairman and CEO James Peck said "We expect about 1/10 of a point of growth from these AI solutions in 2026, which remains a foundation-building year", and that for 2027 "we believe our new AI-native product suite can contribute about 0.25 to 0.5 point of growth" - a figure CFO Mike Burwell repeated verbatim. Peck also said "Revenue from these products is up 28% year-to-date" for BASES AI and Retail Analytics, that "roughly 74% of AI-native revenue comes from clients who bought our solutions in 2025", and that "In less than 6 months, we have reached 10 charter commitments with AI Builder, including Mars Snacking and Purina." Q3 revenue was `$1,119.1 million`, up `6.3%`.

Mechanism
NIQ sells consumer and retail measurement to CPG manufacturers and retailers. The AI layer is sold as new SKUs on top of that data asset: BASES AI for product testing, AI-native Retail Analytics, AI Builder for clients assembling their own agents on NIQ data, an agent built "In partnership with OpenAI DeployCo" starting with US Beauty, and data licensing to Google. Peck sized the addressable pool as a small share of top-100 manufacturer technology budgets that a consulting firm estimated growing from about `$60 billion` in 2024 to about `$90 billion` in 2030, "positions us for $500 million to $1 billion of potential growth".
Why this shape
S2 and not S3, though the S3 reading was tested. AI Builder has no predecessor in NIQ's own catalogue, which is one of METHOD's S3 hunting grounds, but the counterfactual fails: these are existing CPG clients with existing analytics budgets, and absent NIQ they buy comparable work from Circana, Kantar, a consultancy or their own in-house teams - Circana and Kantar lose the sale, so it is a share transfer. The `74%` figure makes this explicit: three-quarters of AI-native revenue comes from clients already on the books. The reason to log it is the calibration, not the shape: a `$4.5bn`-revenue incumbent that is unusually bullish on AI narrative - a `$500m`-`$1bn` opportunity, an OpenAI partnership, named charter clients - puts AI's actual 2026 contribution to its own growth rate at `0.1` of a percentage point. That is the gap between AI capex narrative and realized enterprise revenue, quantified by the company making the narrative.

Booking Holdings Travel / online travel agency · US S1 — Velocity

Direct test of the falsifier written into this log's 2026-08-10 Booking entry, and the second limb fired: the company that refused agentic conversion numbers in Q2 disclosed three of them in Q3. On the Q3 2026 call (3 Nov 2026) CFO Ewout Steenbergen said "Priceline's Penny and Agoda's AI Assistant users have converted at approximately twice the rate of non-users", that "Agoda's AI Assistant users have an average booking value of approximately 20% higher than non-users", and that "Booking.com's daily AI search visitors are up 10x with trip search in the third quarter compared to the second quarter." The first limb did not fire: "LLM referrals have accelerated over the last several weeks but still account for significantly less than 1%".

Mechanism
Two AI assistants sit inside the booking funnel - Penny on Priceline and Agoda's AI Assistant, now live across 21 markets - taking a traveller from an open-ended prompt to a specific property. Steenbergen framed the economics as three separate levers: engagement, "higher conversion", and "higher booking value". Separately, third-party LLM and agent surfaces (OpenAI, Google, Meta, Anthropic, Amazon, plus OpenTable on Muse) act as an inbound referral channel.
Why this shape
S1: every disclosed number is an input metric - a conversion rate, a basket size, a visitor count - and not one dollar of incremental gross bookings or revenue is attributed to any of them. Revenue conversion is conspicuously absent, and the asymmetry is sharp: the only quantified economics on the call were cost-side, with Fogel on "real efficiency gains we are seeing today" and Steenbergen disclosing that at Agoda "our blended cost per million AI tokens declined by more than 25% between June and September". The 2x and +20% figures are also unadjusted user-vs-non-user comparisons: a traveller who opens an AI assistant has already self-selected for intent, so these are ceilings on any causal lift, and Booking offered no counterfactual, holdout or cohort control. Steenbergen's own framing concedes the gap - he listed what management is watching as "engagement... conversion... booking value... incrementality", naming incrementality as the thing still to be established.

Hiscox Ltd Insurance / specialty P&C · UK / Bermuda S1 — Velocity

Hiscox's Q3 2026 trading statement (3 Nov 2026) says that in US broker "an AI-augmented submission-to-quote solution has improved our speed to quote by around 75%" and that it "also contributed to an improved quote-to-bind ratio" - with no figure of any kind attached to the bind ratio. Mechanism
AI-augmented intake on broker submissions - extraction, triage and pre-population - compressing the elapsed time from a broker emailing a submission to Hiscox returning a quote in US broker lines, trade press reporting the effect concentrated in terrorism and architects and engineers. Revenue conversion is conspicuously absent in the strongest available sense - the premium growth figure sits in the same short document as the AI claim and the company declined to connect them, which is the choice a management team makes when it cannot or will not defend the link. This is the same structure as the specialty-insurer S1 cluster already in this log (Ategrity, Skyward, Neptune, Orion180, Ryan Specialty, AIG): a large, quotable process percentage, and silence where the bound premium should be.

Stantec Inc. Professional services / engineering design · Canada S1 — Velocity⚠ Counter-evidence

A `$1.9bn`-a-quarter engineering consultancy - exactly the kind of firm whose billable hours AI is supposed to compress or expand - told analysts on the Q3 2026 call (13 Nov 2026) that it sees neither effect yet. CEO Susan Reisbord: "We are seeing productivity enhancements relative, like on a broad scale" and "we're implementing, like many people in our industry and others, for example, certain AI tools that can help you put proposals together faster", but "relative to seeing sort of any material impacts to the business, we're still in early days." CFO Vito Culmone, asked by TD Cowen's Michael Tupholme whether AI is affecting pricing: "We get a lot of questions around AI and how's that affecting pricing... It's not, at the end of the day, at this point in time, and we don't expect it to be."

Mechanism
Professional services revenue is hours times rate. AI could raise it (more proposals won, more projects delivered per engineer) or destroy it (clients paying for output, not time, compressing rates). Stantec reports AI landing in proposal drafting and administrative work - the non-billable layer - and explicitly denies a rate effect in either direction.
Why this shape
Counter-evidence tagged to S1. The CEO describes a velocity mechanism (proposals assembled faster) and then withholds the velocity number and denies any material business impact, which is the S1 silence this log keeps recording, stated out loud by management rather than inferred from an omission. The pricing answer is the more valuable half: the professional-services AI thesis in both directions - new billable work that was previously uneconomic (S3) and fee deflation as clients capture the productivity - requires a rate effect, and Stantec's CFO says there is none now and expects none. Set against this log's SPI Research entry (2026-10-23), where `84.8%` of professional-services firms reported time savings and `29.9%` reported revenue gains, Stantec is the named-incumbent version of that gap.
