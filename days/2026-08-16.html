RadNet Healthcare · US S3 candidate (unproven)

On the Q2 2026 call (10 Aug 2026) CFO Mark Stolper said "Revenue from the EBCD program increased 16%" - RadNet's Enhanced Breast Cancer Detection, an AI second read on screening mammograms that patients buy out of pocket because payers do not reimburse it. No absolute dollar figure, adoption rate or counterfactual was given on the call.

Mechanism
A screening mammogram is covered by insurance; the AI over-read is not. RadNet offers the patient an opt-in upcharge (priced at $75 falling to $40 during the DeepHealth study period) for Saige-DX to read the study alongside the radiologist, with a second AI pass and escalation to a second radiologist on suspicious findings. The revenue is patient cash on top of a reimbursed exam - AI inference cost is near zero per study, so the entire upcharge is incremental gross margin on a scan the company was already performing.
Why this shape
(a) Counterfactual purchase - PASS. The buyer is the patient, the purchase is optional, and absent the AI there is nothing to buy: the base exam is already paid for by the payer. (b) Prior alternative - PASS. Nobody sold a consumer an AI over-read before this; the nearest analogue is a radiologist double-read, which is not a purchasable US consumer SKU. The DeepHealth RSNA 2024 analysis of 747,604 women across 10 practices recorded 23% enrolment rising to 33% in the final month and states plainly that "AI is not reimbursed" - the closest published evidence that this is net-new self-pay demand. (c) Capability gate - FAIL, and this is the honest weak point. Saige-DX is CNN-era FDA-cleared mammography AI, not a post-2024 frontier capability; 2021 technology would have worked. (d) Newly economic - PASS. A human second read cannot be delivered at $40; near-zero marginal inference cost is what opens the price point. Held at S3-CANDIDATE rather than promoted: the Q2 2026 disclosure is a growth rate with no denominator, management never framed it as incremental demand, and (c) fails outright. It is nonetheless the first entry in this log where the counterfactual purchase question has a defensible yes.

CarGurus Media / Marketplaces (autos) · US S1 — Velocity

On the Q2 2026 call (6 Aug 2026) CEO Jason Trevisan said "Guru-driven leads up 60% sequentially in the U.S." and that VINMax "promoted listings have sold 23% faster and received 34% more leads per day than comparable non-promoted listings"; President & COO Sam Zales said "PriceVantage bookings grew more than 50% sequentially" and that adopting dealers "saw a median 15% lift in VDPs and 9% lift in leads per listing after adoption". Total revenue was $251.0m, up 13%.

Mechanism
Two distinct paths. Consumer side: Guru is a natural-language shopping layer that lets buyers describe a use case rather than filter a taxonomy, converting browsing into dealer leads. Dealer side: PriceVantage uses demand signal to tell a dealer where market demand for specific vehicles sits, and VINMax decides which listings to promote. Dealers pay a subscription, so the revenue path runs lead volume -> perceived value -> higher average revenue per subscribing dealer (QARSD), not through a transaction take rate.
Why this shape
Every disclosed number is an input metric - leads, vehicle detail page views, days-to-sale, bookings - so S1 by test. Revenue conversion was disclosed only obliquely and never in dollars: the Q2 2026 earnings presentation states new product adoption was the largest driver of sequential QARSD increases for a third consecutive quarter, and total revenue grew 13%, but no AI-attributed revenue figure was given and full-year growth guidance was left unchanged at 10-13%. Treated as main log on the precedent already set by Cars.com and Zillow in this log; the marketplace-vs-tech-platform boundary is genuinely arguable here. Note the 60% and 50% figures are sequential, not year-over-year - a favourable framing that flatters small bases.

RadNet (DeepHealth / Digital Health segment) Healthcare · US S2 — Share

On the Q2 2026 call (10 Aug 2026) Digital Health CEO Kees Westdorp disclosed "$16.1 million of AI revenue, up 136% year-over-year", Digital Health revenue of "$32.4 million, up 56.5%", ARR of "$105.5 million, up 97%", and that "External ARR, revenue generated outside of RadNet, now makes up 63% of our ARR base", guiding full-year ARR to over $140m.

Mechanism
An imaging provider productised the AI it built for its own reading workflow and now licenses it to competing hospitals and health systems - Diagnostic Suite, Reporting Pro, AI Studio and clinical AI models - signing approximately $21m of total contract value in the quarter. Nearly two thirds of recurring revenue now comes from outside RadNet's own centres.
Why this shape
Routed to the appendix because at this segment RadNet is a software vendor, not a deploying corporation: it is selling the shovels, and METHOD excludes shovel-sellers as subjects of the revenue log even when the parent sits in an in-scope sector. Shape is S2, not S3: the hospitals buying DeepHealth were already buying imaging AI and reporting software from iCAD, Lunit, Aidoc, Sectra and Philips, so a competitor lost the sale. The interesting thing is the direction of travel - a non-tech incumbent crossing into vendor economics off its own deployment - which is exactly the comparator the appendix exists to preserve. Note the segment's adjusted EBITDA fell to $2.5m from $3.4m a year earlier on infrastructure investment: the AI vendor line is growing revenue and shrinking profit.
