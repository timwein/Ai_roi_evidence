The pattern hardens and now has a name: the attribution gap is not shy, it is structural. Five runs in, the tape splits three ways with almost no overlap. Non-tech incumbents talk about AI and attribute nothing (Aon, ADP, L'Oreal, Ategrity). Non-tech incumbents post excellent revenue numbers and attribute them to discipline, pricing and mix rather than AI (Frontier +38%, Quest +10.2%, GM's OnStar line on 28 Jul). And the only parties putting large, auditable AI revenue figures on the record are the vendors selling it - ServiceNow $1bn of AI ACV, Infosys 8.2% of revenue. Someone's $1bn is someone else's unattributed cost line.

Ategrity Specialty Insurance Company Holdings Insurance · US S1 — Velocity

On the Q2 2026 call (29 Jul 2026) management said the new Architects & Engineers product was "brought to market earlier this month using what we estimate to be approximately 60% fewer resources because of AI," and that the company has "all but eliminated" the fixed cost of launching a new product. Q2 gross written premium was more than $205m, up 22%+, with no premium attributed to AI or broken out for the new lines.

Mechanism
Product-launch capacity, not underwriting. Ategrity uses AI (including agentic tooling across marketing and governance) to build and file a new specialty line - forms, rating, appetite, distribution collateral - with roughly 60% of the previous internal resource. The revenue path is that a carrier which previously could only afford to stand up large classes can now stand up smaller ones, so the addressable set of classes widens. The premium consequence is entirely undisclosed.
Why this shape
S1, not S3, and the distinction is the whole point. The disclosed number is an input metric (resources consumed to launch), never revenue. On the S3 tests: (a) counterfactual purchase fails - an architects-and-engineers buyer would have bought A&E cover from one of many existing carriers; (b) prior alternative fails for the same reason, this is an old, competitively written class, so any premium won here is taken from a competitor; (c) capability gate is arguable at best - form drafting and filing support does not obviously require a post-2024 frontier model; (d) newly economic is the only test with any pull, and only for Ategrity's own economics, not for the segment. Revenue conversion was conspicuously NOT disclosed: the 10 Jul pre-announcement says "recently launched products... began contributing meaningfully" and attaches no dollar figure, and the call attributes none of the 22%+ premium growth to AI. Note also that this is the closest any incumbent in this log has come to articulating the METHOD cost-collapse-opens-a-segment mechanism in its own words - "all but eliminated the fixed cost of launching a new product" is the S3 pathway stated without the S3 evidence.
