The pattern of the week, and it is new: four large non-tech companies were asked the same question by the same programme in the same news cycle, and the answers sort cleanly by how exposed the speaker was. Santander gave a composite euro figure and refused to decompose it. Standard Chartered's CFO pointed at the top line on television while its CEO had said on the transcript that the effect has not yet arrived. Novartis's CEO said "tangible" with no number, in a week when his own filed release mentioned AI twice in a director's CV. Carlsberg's CEO said not at scale. Only the last of those is falsifiable in the direction of modesty, and it is the only one that costs the speaker nothing to be wrong about.
Second, and it extends a pattern now three runs old: metric rotation is the house style. CarGurus became the third company in this log to replace an entire set of AI statistics rather than update them - all four Q2 figures gone, four new ones in, engagement still framed sequentially while revenue is framed year-over-year. Ryan Specialty has now done this three quarters running and Customers Bancorp migrated from revenue to cost inside one quarter. A statistic that is never repeated cannot be falsified, which is what it is for.
Third, the one the capex debate should sit with: the best-instrumented deployer in this log quantified its AI benefit to the nearest CAD 25m and attributed none of it to revenue. Intact has 600-plus models in production, prices 94% of Canadian personal lines with machine learning, measures the benefit on controlled samples, and reports the whole of it as loss ratio and expense ratio. If an insurer that far down the curve sees no top line, the burden of proof on firms claiming AI revenue with less apparatus should go up, not down. Note also the two caveats inside Intact's own number: Barbeau said continued AI investment "will fund some of it", and the margin claim is outperformance against industry, not improvement on its own book - the Q3 combined ratio was 94.0% against 89.8% a year earlier.

AirSculpt Technologies, Inc. Healthcare / elective procedures · US S2 — Share⚠ Counter-evidence

The only quantified AI top-line attribution from a non-tech incumbent this week carries a minus sign. On the Q3 2026 call (6 Nov 2026) CEO Yogesh Jashnani said queries featuring AI overviews "have now risen to nearly 90% of healthcare-related queries", producing "fewer Google clicks because Google increasingly keeps consumers on its own platform" and "16% lower leads versus prior year and 26% higher cost per lead in the quarter", "impacting both revenue and profitability". CFO Michael Autor said of the marketing cost increase that "that increase is primarily driven by AI overviews, which impacted traffic and leads reaching our website in the period". Revenue fell 8.7% to $31.9m, same-centre case volume fell 9.6%, and FY26 revenue guidance was cut to $144m-$146m from $151m-$157m with adjusted EBITDA cut to $9m-$10m from $12m-$14m.

Mechanism
AirSculpt buys essentially all of its demand through paid and organic search: a consumer searches a body-contouring term, clicks through, becomes a lead, and converts to a cash-pay procedure averaging roughly $13k. Google's AI Overviews answer the query on Google's own page, so the click never happens. The funnel loses volume at the top and the remaining clicks are bid up, which is why leads fell 16% while cost per lead rose 26% in the same quarter.
Why this shape
S2 by the share test, read from the losing side. Absent AI Overviews these consumers were still in-market for body contouring and most will buy from somebody - a competitor, a local plastic surgeon, or AirSculpt later through a different channel. Demand was reallocated, not destroyed, so this is zero-sum at industry level and GDP-neutral, exactly like the S2 wins logged here from the winning side. Not S3 and not an inverse-S3: nothing about total category demand is disclosed. The important asymmetry is evidentiary rather than economic - this company put a hard, dated, guidance-moving number on AI's effect on its own revenue line, which almost none of the 50 S2 entries in this log have done for the positive case. Note also what is absent: the filed 8-K results release mentions AI nowhere. The entire attribution lives on the call.

A CEO and a CFO at the same bank gave opposite accounts of whether AI is in the top line, three days apart. On the Q3 2026 results call (28 Oct 2026, income +14% to $5.8bn, profit before tax +29% to $2.3bn, expenses +9%, FY guidance upgraded to above the 5%-7% income range and RoTE to around 14%) Group CEO Bill Winters said of the Wealth franchise: "this is sort of all before we get the full effect of AI. Mechanism
The claimed path is relationship-manager productivity in the affluent and Wealth Solutions business: AI tools raise the quality and consistency of RM advice, which raises income per client. Why this shape
S2 by shape if the claim were real - affluent banking income taken from other private banks in the same Asian and Middle Eastern markets, a competitor losing the mandate. It is logged as counter-evidence because the two statements cannot both be true. The CEO's formulation is the more exposed of the two: he is on a transcript his own investor-relations team published, and he says the revenue effect is still ahead of the company. The CFO's is on television. Where a bank's own Tier A document says the AI revenue effect has not yet arrived and its CFO tells a broadcaster you can already see it, the log records the transcript and flags the gap. Axon Enterprise, Inc. Public-safety equipment and software (out of scope as a subject - sells AI as product) · US S3 candidate (unproven)

The strongest new-TAM-shaped revenue number retrieved this run, and it is out of scope. The Q3 2026 shareholder letter filed as 8-K Exhibit 99.1 (5 Nov 2026) states "AI bookings up over 50% year to date; AI ARR surpasses $100 million" and "AI annual recurring revenue surpassed $100 million." On the call CEO Patrick Smith said "Our AI products have already passed $100 million in annual recurring revenue" and "AI became a meaningful part of it in less than 2 years"; CFO Brittany Bagley added "that $100 million of ARR still has a ton of opportunity ahead of it." Against total ARR of $1.75bn (+40%), revenue of $909m (+28%), Software & Services of $437m (+43%) and net revenue retention of 126%. The letter does not define what counts in AI ARR.

Mechanism
Axon sells police agencies a bundled software plan, the AI Era Plan, layered on an installed base of body cameras, fleet cameras and the Axon Evidence records system. The AI SKUs inside it have no predecessor in Axon's own catalogue: Draft One drafts incident narratives from body-camera audio, Axon Assistant does real-time translation at the point of contact, and Axon Guardian - newly generally available - "introduces a new category of field intelligence", detecting escalation signals from a recording Body 4 or Body 5 camera and vehicle-door opening on Fleet 3.
Why this shape
Appendix, because the subject is out of scope in the only way that matters: this $100m is Axon selling AI, not Axon earning revenue from AI it deployed in its own revenue process. It is shovel revenue, and the precedent is the Heartflow entry of 2026-09-03. The four tests, run anyway because the shape is the most S3-like thing retrieved this week. (c) Capability gate: passes cleanly - narrative generation from unstructured body-camera audio and real-time escalation inference did not exist as products 24 months ago, and the company itself dates the business at under two years. (b) Prior alternative: passes for Draft One and Assistant - the prior alternative was an officer typing a report at shift end, an unpaid manual workaround, with no competing product to lose the sale. (d) Newly economic: passes - these were technically impossible rather than merely expensive. (a) Counterfactual purchase: FAILS, which is why this is S3-CANDIDATE and not S3. The missing evidence is specific and the letter supplies the reason to doubt: AI ARR is reported as part of "continued adoption of premium software offerings, including the AI Era Plan", i.e. bundle upsell priced into accounts that already buy Axon Evidence - which is S2, upsell into the installed base, not new demand. With no definition of AI ARR and no SKU-level split, the share that is genuinely incremental cannot be established. The tell is Draft One itself, the clearest no-predecessor product in the bundle: Smith said the prosecutor sign-off requirement "maybe slowed down Draft One a bit" because it "creates documents that then have to go over to prosecutors and the courts". The flagship new-TAM SKU is the one that is not scaling.

Intact Financial Corporation Insurance · Canada C4 — Non-tech · non-engineering

The largest quantified AI benefit figure any insurer has put in this log, and every dollar of it is margin. On the Q3 2026 call (4 Nov 2026) CEO Charles Brindamour said "We earn today close to CAD 250 million in recurring pre-tax earnings" from AI and that Intact expects to "double that in the next 24 months from our AI deployment", with "a substantial runway beyond that". COO Patrick Barbeau split the benefit: "around 60% of that" improves the loss ratio and "the other part will help the expense ratio". Brindamour: "There's no doubt that AI has already expanded our margins, and that'll continue and accelerate." Deployment depth disclosed alongside it: "more than 600 of our own models in productions", "Machine learning prices 94% of our Canadian personal lines business and 75% of our commercial lines", advanced pricing models covering 50% of global specialty premium. Not one dollar of revenue or premium is attributed to AI.

Mechanism
Two cost paths, both in the combined ratio. Loss ratio: machine-learning pricing and risk selection put the right price on the right risk, plus fraud detection and reduced indemnity leakage in claims - Brindamour said it "sharpens our fraud detection, reduces indemnity leakage, and helps our adjusters and lawyers close files faster". Expense ratio: automated commercial-lines quote submission cut broker turnaround by 70% in Canada, and legal-claims document summarization prep time by roughly 50%.
Why this shape
C4 - non-tech, non-engineering, and the cleanest example in the ledger of why the cost and revenue ledgers are kept apart. This is a sophisticated deployer that has instrumented its AI benefit to the nearest CAD 25m, quantified it in a controlled-sample methodology, broken it into loss ratio and expense ratio, and attributed exactly zero of it to the top line. Better pricing on risks Intact was already writing is not new demand and not share taken on product merit; it is the cost of claims falling. Two cautions recorded as required. Trap 6, the run-cost offset, fired verbatim: Barbeau said "We want to continue to invest in AI going forward, so that will fund some of it", so part of the CAD 250m gross is consumed by the AI spend producing it. And the margin claim is relative, not absolute - the Q3 combined ratio was 94.0%, worse than 89.8% a year earlier, while Barbeau argued "it will expand our combined ratio outperformance over the next two years" and "the expansion of the outperformance is bigger than what we measure". Outperformance against the Canadian industry, not margin improvement on its own book.
