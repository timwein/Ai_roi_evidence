Assessment

Four findings, no S3 for the tenth consecutive run, and three of the four are negative. The week's signal is that two retailers which have built AI directly into the buying path were each asked what drove their online growth and named something else.

This run covers the seven days since 2026-11-20. Late-November reporting is thin and non-tech: Deere's FY2026 close, Best Buy, Kingfisher and Compass Group. The pattern, now in its third form: last week non-tech retailers published a conversion multiple with no denominator. This week two of them published neither. Best Buy and Kingfisher both sell through AI surfaces, both grew online double-digit, and both attributed that growth to supply-side causes - marketplace sellers and trade - with AI absent from the answer rather than merely unsized.

That is a more useful week than another multiple would have been, because the two cases rule out different things. Best Buy is the strongest available test of third-party agentic distribution: checkout embedded in two frontier assistants, an own-brand assistant live, a direct analyst question, and no number of any kind - not even a usage metric of the sort Walmart and Target volunteer.

Kingfisher is the first case in this log of a company retiring a revenue-attributed AI figure it had previously promoted. The c.£100m was framed at the half year as demonstrating "the growing value of our data and digital capabilities"; its growth rate had already decelerated from +40% in FY 25/26 to +16% in H1 26/27. March's full-year RNS has an established slot for the number, which makes this a clean falsifier to check rather than an inference.

Deere is the cost-side mirror. The only quantified benefit anywhere in its AI story - herbicide savings "of more than 50%" - accrues to the farmer, and the two-quarter-old falsifier written here on 2026-09-02 asked whether Deere would ever break out precision-ag revenue. It still has not, and an analyst had to raise monetisation for management to address it at all.

Best Buy Co., Inc. Retail / Consumer electronics · US S2 — Share⚠ Counter-evidence

The most agentic-commerce-integrated retailer in this log attributed none of its growth to it. On the Q3 FY2027 call (24 Nov 2026) CEO Jason Bonfig said "Ask Blue, our conversational AI shopping and support assistant is live on our site and app" and that "The Best Buy catalog and expertise are accessible inside of ChatGPT, Gemini and Muse, and we've integrated checkout directly into ChatGPT and Gemini so customers can go from questions to purchase without ever leaving the platform that they're in". Piper Sandler's Peter Keith then asked what was behind domestic online comparable sales of +10.8% - "you did mention some partnership with various AI platforms. What might be driving that online acceleration?" - and Bonfig's answer named only Marketplace and first-party: "Marketplace is a big part of that… for the first time, we actually were able to have international sellers on our Marketplace… That combination of Marketplace… with a really strong 1P business is really what's driving our digital sales". Enterprise comparable sales were +6.1%, "our highest in five years", on revenue of $10,236m, with Marketplace GMV of approximately $350m in the quarter.

Mechanism
Two routes, both live and neither sized. Ask Blue is Best Buy's own conversational assistant on its site and app, the standard personalisation/conversion mechanism. The second is third-party agentic distribution: Best Buy's catalogue is exposed inside ChatGPT, Gemini and Meta's Muse, with checkout embedded in the first two, so a sale can complete without the customer reaching bestbuy.com. The quarter's actual disclosed revenue drivers are a marketplace supply expansion (international sellers added for the first time) and product cycles in computing above the $1,500 price point.
Why this shape
Counter-evidence tagged to S2, the shape it contradicts: if an assistant plus embedded checkout in two frontier chat platforms produced incremental share, this is the quarter and the question in which a CEO would have said so. He did not, and the company's own written release mentions AI only as "with the strategic use of artificial intelligence" and as a product category - "such as AI glasses and trading cards". Not logged as a positive S1 because no AI input metric was disclosed either: no Ask Blue usage, no agentic order count, no conversion differential - a notable contrast with Walmart, Target, Lowe's and Booking, all of which published a conversion multiple. The emerging-category strength Bonfig did quantify - "AI glasses, health rings and trading cards are kind of the three biggest categories there" - is AI-as-product-sold, not ROI on Best Buy's own AI deployment, and is excluded on that basis.

Kingfisher plc Retail / Home improvement · UK S2 — Share

A hard revenue-attributed AI figure from a non-tech incumbent, and then its disappearance eight weeks later. Kingfisher's H1 26/27 results RNS (22 Sep 2026) states: "AI-driven personalisation generated c.£100m of sales, up +16%, demonstrating the growing value of our data and digital capabilities", alongside e-commerce sales of £1,555m at 21.9% penetration. The Q3 26/27 trading update to 31 October 2026, published 24 Nov 2026, contains no mention of AI, personalisation or recommendation anywhere in the document: group sales £3,257m (+0.2% reported, underlying LFL +0.8%), e-commerce sales growth +15% ex-Screwfix with penetration at 22%, marketplace GMV +36% to £170m, and adjusted PBT guidance upgraded to c.£610m-£640m from £595m-£635m. CEO Thierry Garnier credited "continued market share gains and strong momentum across our strategic growth drivers of trade, e-commerce and marketplace".

Mechanism
Recommendation and personalisation engines on Kingfisher's digital channels (over 1bn annual visits across B&Q, Screwfix, Castorama and Brico Depot), measured as sales flowing through personalised surfaces. Kingfisher separately runs AI on the productivity side - "AI-enabled tools such as Fabric, accelerating content creation, enhancing search engine optimisation and improving conversion" - and in H1 flagged that it is "preparing for the next phase of digital commerce, including natural language search and agent-enabled shopping experiences".
Why this shape
S2, and the company does not pretend otherwise: a customer buying paint or building materials through a personalised recommendation would have bought paint or building materials, from Kingfisher or from a competitor. Absent the AI this is a share question, not a demand question, so the gain is zero-sum at industry level and GDP-neutral. Fails every S3 test: no counterfactual non-purchase, the prior alternative is an unpersonalised Kingfisher page or a rival's, recommendation engines long predate the ~24-month capability gate, and no uneconomic segment is opened. The number is also a company self-attribution with no disclosed methodology, and c.£100m is about 1.4% of H1 group sales of £6.9bn - which is roughly the order of magnitude of every other precisely-quantified AI revenue claim in this log (Etsy at 2 points of GMS growth, NIQ at 0.1 of a point). The load-bearing observation is the sequence: a figure that was volunteered and framed as proof of "the growing value of our data and digital capabilities" at the half year is entirely absent from the next scheduled disclosure, in which e-commerce growth of +15% is credited to trade, e-commerce and marketplace instead.

Deere & Company Industrials / Agricultural equipment · US S1 — Velocity⚠ Counter-evidence

Direct test of the falsifier written into this log's 2026-09-02 Deere entry, and both limbs fired. On the Q4 FY2026 call (25 Nov 2026) Chairman and CEO John May launched Deere's first general-purpose AI product - "Introducing JD, the first AI assistant, is now in use around the world" - and sized it purely in usage: "In the first week of full availability, more than 6,000 users in 76 countries held more than 10,000 conversations with JD". IR director Chris Seibert said See & Spray "covered approximately 12 million acres in 2026" globally against "5 million acres of utilization in the U.S. and Canada" last year, and held "roughly one-third of orders in our recently concluded sprayer Early Order Program"; May said Brazil take rates are "running at roughly twice, two times the level we saw during the first year in North America". No revenue, price premium or margin figure was attached to any of it, Deere still does not break out precision-ag revenue, and Production & Precision Ag Q4 net sales fell 15% to $4bn with full-year segment sales down about 9%.

Mechanism
Two distinct mechanisms, neither priced. JD is a natural-language assistant inside Operations Center that lets growers query field and machine history; Deere charges nothing identified for it and Seibert framed it as "an evolution of our digital infrastructure in Operations Center". See & Spray is machine-vision weed detection sold as a factory-installed sprayer option, so its revenue path is option attach rate and price mix on equipment Deere already sells; the disclosed benefit - herbicide savings "of more than 50%" - accrues to the farmer, not to Deere.
Why this shape
S1: every disclosed number is an input metric - users, conversations, acres, order attach rate, take-rate ratio - and no revenue is attributed. Revenue conversion is not merely absent, it was raised by an analyst and deferred. Wells Fargo's Jerry Revich put it directly: "AI wasn't a big part of the framework at the last Analyst Day in terms of the incremental revenue opportunity set" and asked "how we could potentially monetize the additional capability set that has evolved since". Seibert's answer was "we want to give the customer the opportunity to get more out of their dataset" and that management would "talk a little bit more about that in the future". On precision ag he allowed only that "There will be a recurring revenue component of that" - future tense, unsized. Not S3 on the four tests: (a) a farmer buying a sprayer buys a sprayer regardless and JD is bundled into a platform growers already have; (b) the prior alternative for JD is reading the same Operations Center data manually, an unpaid workaround that was never a market anyone sold into, which is why there is also no price; (c) a conversational assistant over proprietary agronomic data does clear the ~24-month capability gate, but with no SKU and no price there is no revenue to classify; (d) no previously uneconomic segment is opened - the same farms are served. CFO Brent Norwood called the Orchard AI acquisition "a significant inorganic growth lever for us", which is M&A framing, not deployment ROI.
