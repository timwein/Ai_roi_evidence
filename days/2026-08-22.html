InterContinental Hotels Group (IHG) Hospitality · UK S2 — Share

On the H1 2026 results call (11 Aug 2026) CEO Elie Maalouf cited "the rollout of our AI-enhanced revenue management system, which has unlocked revenue uplift and market share gains for our owners." No magnitude was attached, and the H1 results announcement published the same day contains no mention of AI at all. Group RevPAR was +4.1% and operating profit +10% to $665m; neither was attributed to AI.

Mechanism
An AI-enhanced revenue management system setting room rates across franchised and managed hotels. The uplift accrues to hotel owners as RevPAR and index gain; IHG monetises it indirectly through percentage-of-revenue fees. Separately in the half Maalouf said IHG "launched new AI-enabled natural language search capabilities within our award-winning mobile app and our website" and "launched an IHG plug-in within ChatGPT", both without any disclosed booking or conversion metric.
Why this shape
S2 on the CEO's own wording. "Market share gains for our owners" is a RevPAR-index claim: the index is measured against a competitive set, so the gain is taken from the hotel across the road. Absent the system the guest still books a room somewhere, so the counterfactual purchase happens - fails S3 test (a). Fails test (c) as well: yield management by machine learning is a 1990s hospitality technology, not a capability that appeared in the last 24 months. Zero-sum at industry level and GDP-neutral.

Carnegie Mellon University / Larridin - 'Do AI Adoption Signals Predict Company Performance?' (564 companies, 538 after dedup, 478 10-K filings, 30,000+ job postings) Cross-sector (research) · US S2 — Share⚠ Counter-evidence

Cross-sectional study surfaced 17 Aug 2026 testing whether public AI-adoption signals - vendor tracker scores, AI hiring intensity, investment intensity and 10-K disclosure language - predict revenue growth, margin improvement or stock returns across 538 companies in 12 sectors. Only one signal survived: narrative concreteness, where firms at the top of the distribution are associated with 8.0 percentage points higher year-over-year revenue growth than those at the bottom (coefficient +0.080, p=0.009). Broader adoption measures weakened under multiple-testing correction, adoption showed little connection to margins, and no public AI signal predicted four-month forward stock returns. AI infrastructure suppliers beat sector- and size-matched peers by roughly 32 points over four months.

Mechanism
No deployment mechanism - this is an attempt to price the deployment mechanism from the outside, using signals an investor can actually observe. That is what makes the null result load-bearing: if AI adoption were producing revenue at scale, the observable proxies for adoption should carry some of it.
Why this shape
Filed against S2 because that is the shape it tests and fails to confirm - attributed incremental revenue on existing products at adopting firms. Counter-evidence, with one important qualification: the surviving coefficient is on how specifically a company *describes* its AI, not on how much AI it has. That is at least as consistent with reverse causality - firms already growing have concrete things to describe - as with AI causing growth, and the paper's own signals cannot separate the two. The infrastructure-supplier result is the picks-and-shovels finding restated: the measurable return sits with the sellers, not the deployers.
