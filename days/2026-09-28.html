Accenture, 'How insurers drive revenue by deploying AI with intent' (263 senior insurance executives: 89 P&C, 100 life/annuity, 74 multi-line) Insurance / Cross-sector research · Global S2 — Share⚠ Counter-evidence

Survey of 263 senior insurance executives with direct accountability for AI, data, technology and transformation across the Americas, Europe and Asia-Pacific, fielded October-November 2025, plus 15 executive interviews: "81% of insurers surveyed are seeing at least a 5% improvement in gross written premiums from AI and data initiatives", with 7% reporting improvements of more than 20%, "driven by better pricing, personalization and cross-selling". The same report finds only 23% have achieved enterprise-wide AI integration, only 32% place revenue growth and business expansion among their top three reasons for investing in AI, and 83% acknowledge moderate-to-severe gaps in connecting AI capability to measurable business outcomes. No individual insurer is named with any figure.

Mechanism
Not a deployment finding - a base-rate measurement of self-reported revenue attribution in the sector this log samples most heavily, and the first one framed on a premium denominator rather than on a vague 'revenue growth' yes/no. Gross written premium is the right denominator for insurance, which is what makes the number worth logging at all: a 5% GWP threshold is a magnitude, not a sentiment.
Why this shape
S2, and counter-evidence. S2 because the stated mechanisms - better pricing, personalization, cross-selling - are share-shifting on existing products in existing segments: absent the AI those policies get written by a competitor, so nothing here is GDP-additive and none of the four S3 tests is even approached. Counter-evidence because the report contradicts itself on the only question this log asks. If 81% of insurers really had at least 5% more gross written premium from AI, that is an industry-scale result no listed carrier has put in a filing; and the same 263 executives report 83% moderate-to-severe gaps in connecting AI to measurable business outcomes, which means the 81% is a self-attribution produced by the same population that admits it cannot measure the thing. The 32% is the third tell: a revenue benefit that large would not rank outside the top three investment objectives for two-thirds of the industry.

Revolve Group, Inc. Retail / Apparel e-commerce · US S1 — Velocity

In an investor deck filed as Form 8-K Exhibit 99.1 for its 3 Jun 2026 William Blair Growth Stock Conference appearance, Revolve states under the heading "AI Is Already Driving Measurable Results Across the Business": "We developed AI search algorithms that drove a double-digit percentage lift in conversion rate and saved hundreds of thousands of dollars in vendor costs annually." Two further features are credited without magnitude - an AI virtual try-on driving "higher conversion rates and lower return rates among shoppers who engage with the tool", and an internally developed generative-AI product-page Q&A "driving a meaningful lift in conversion rate". The same deck reports Q1 2026 net sales up 16%, "the highest growth rate in nearly four years", and attributes no part of it to AI.

Mechanism
Three separate on-site conversion mechanisms, all built in-house. (1) Proprietary AI relevance ranking replaced a paid third-party site-search vendor, so shoppers who search find a buyable item more often. (2) A virtual try-on that raises purchase confidence and, by cutting returns, raises net revenue per order rather than gross. (3) Generative-AI Q&A surfaced on product pages, removing the pre-purchase question that otherwise ends the session. Each acts on conversion of already-acquired traffic, not on traffic volume or on price.
Why this shape
S1, not S2. Conversion rate is named in METHOD's S1 test as an input metric, and Revolve discloses no revenue, no basket effect and no attributed share of its 16% net sales growth. The absence is the interesting part: this is a deck whose own heading claims "measurable results", reporting the fastest sales growth in four years, and it declines to put a single dollar or basis point of that growth against AI - the standing pattern of this log, from a company that had every incentive to close the loop in front of a growth-stock investor audience. One ledger note: the first sentence pairs the conversion claim with a cost saving ("hundreds of thousands of dollars in vendor costs annually"), which is a C4-shaped figure; per METHOD's single-ledger rule this is logged once, as S1, because the conversion lift is what the entry is about, and the vendor saving is in any case immaterial against Revolve's cost base.
