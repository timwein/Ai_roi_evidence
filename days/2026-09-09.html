Health In Tech, Inc. Insurance / health benefits marketplace · US S3 candidate (unproven)⚠ Counter-evidence

In its Q2 2026 results release, filed as an SEC Form 8-K exhibit dated 13 Aug 2026, Health In Tech reported quarterly revenue of $8.1 million against $9.3 million in Q2 2025, and first-half revenue of $16.8 million against $17.3 million. It reaffirmed full-year 2026 revenue guidance of $45 million to $50 million, which arithmetically requires H2 revenue of $28.2m to $33.2m against the $16.8m delivered in H1. Supporting figures disclosed: contracted revenue of $32.3 million as of 30 June 2026, pipeline revenue of $66.3 million as of 31 July 2026, and 933 distribution partners, up 19.9% year over year. On the call the same day CEO Tim Johnson said of the decline: "This was not a demand problem. This was a churn problem and was not a platform problem. It was a timing factor tied to onboarding a new carrier into our platform." Logged as counter-evidence: this is the cleanest S3-shaped business model in insurance - AI-underwritten stop-loss sold to employers too small for the traditional self-funded market - and its revenue went backwards year over year in both the quarter and the half. The disclosure is 27 days old, outside this log's normal window, and is logged because it is unlogged, primary-sourced and cuts against the thesis.

Mechanism
The eDIYBS marketplace takes a broker's census and claims-experience files, parses them without a human underwriter, applies a carrier's own criteria configured in-system, and returns a bindable, execution-ready stop-loss quote in minutes rather than days. Revenue is earned as recurring monthly administrative fees on enrolled plans plus a percentage of stop-loss premium underwritten. The asserted new-TAM mechanism is that automated underwriting collapses the cost of quoting a very small case far enough that self-funded plans, historically an option only for larger employers, become sellable to small ones. HitRix, announced for H2 2026, extends the same document-intelligence layer across the wider stop-loss ecosystem.
Why this shape
S3-CANDIDATE, appendix, and counter-evidence. Test (a) counterfactual purchase: management asserts it serves small employers who previously could not access self-funded plans, but the release evidences no such buyer - there is no split between employers converting from fully-insured cover and employers buying for the first time. Test (b) prior alternative: the default alternative for a small employer is a fully-insured small-group policy from an existing carrier, which is a real purchase from somebody; on the evidence disclosed this reads as S2 against incumbent small-group carriers, not S3, and that is precisely the evidence that is missing. Test (c) capability gate: document parsing of census and experience files is plausible without a post-2024 frontier model, so the gate is not clearly met. Test (d) newly economic: asserted, never quantified - no cost-per-quote figure, no floor on case size that moved. Out of scope as a subject because Health In Tech is an AI-native marketplace platform selling the underwriting technology itself, so it goes to the appendix and is never counted. The reason it is worth logging at all is METHOD's point that entrants quantify what incumbents will not: here the entrant quantified, and the number went down.
