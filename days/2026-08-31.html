BMO Financial Group (BMO Insurance) Banking / life insurance · Canada S1 — Velocity

On the Q3 2026 earnings call (25 Aug 2026) CEO Darryl White said: "BMO Insurance launched another AI-powered platform, SmartDecision. It uses predictive modeling to deliver underwriting decisions in as little as 10 seconds, compared with an industry average of 28 business days or more." BMO's own launch release (9 Jul 2026) puts instant decisions on eligible applications up to $5 million across Term Life, Universal Life and Whole Life, with no traditional tele-interviews and no medical evidence for eligible applicants. No premium, no policy count, no revenue and no attach-rate figure was attached to SmartDecision on the call or in the release.

Mechanism
Predictive models score eligible life applications against third-party and application data in place of tele-interview and paramedical evidence, so an advisor can bind coverage inside a single client meeting instead of losing the applicant across a 28-business-day requirements cycle. The revenue path, if it exists, is fall-out reduction: applications that currently die between illustration and issue.
Why this shape
S1, not S2 or S3. The disclosed number is latency - `10 seconds` against an industry `28 business days` - a pure input metric, so it cannot be S2 without attributed dollars. Revenue conversion was conspicuously absent: White cited it in a list of AI proof points and neither he nor CFO commentary sized bound premium, application volume, or individual-insurance sales growth against it. Against the S3 tests it fails (a) and (b): a buyer seeking $5m of term coverage would have bought it from another Canadian carrier on the slower process, so any gain is share taken from Manulife, Sun Life or Canada Life, not new demand. Test (c) is also weak - accelerated underwriting on third-party data predates 2024. The one live S3 question BMO did not answer is whether instant no-medical issue converts applicants who abandon the traditional process entirely; nothing in the disclosure establishes that.

Meta Platforms Technology - internet platform · US C1 — Tech · engineering⚠ Counter-evidence

A Reuters special report (26 Aug 2026, Katie Paul), built on internal documents, recordings and 20+ interviews, reports that Meta cancelled the November wave of layoffs under "Project OT" (Organization Transformation) - a plan to hand the daily work of thousands of employees to AI agents supervised by small teams of human "builders" - after internal data showed code changes to Meta's own platforms and infrastructure up 220% year over year while changes producing new or upgraded user-visible features rose only 36%. Technical and security incidents rose 40% and time spent on incident response rose 70%. Employee sentiment fell from 74% to 55% favorable. Zuckerberg told staff AI agent technology had not "accelerated" as quickly as he had anticipated. The May 2026 10% reduction stands; the second wave does not.

Mechanism
Agentic coding and workflow agents replacing human execution across engineering and adjacent functions, with keystroke-and-click tracking software installed on US employees' devices to train agents to reproduce human workflows. The failure mode reported is not that agents produced nothing - they produced a great deal of code - but that the output did not reach users.
Why this shape
C1 counter-evidence, and it lands directly on this log's own 2026-01-28 Meta entry, where CFO Susan Li disclosed `30%` higher output per engineer and `80%` for power users. This is the first quantified read of what that throughput converted into: a `220%` rise in code changes against a `36%` rise in shipped user-visible features, with incidents up `40%` and firefighting time up `70%`. That is the denominator the January figure lacked, and it is the cleanest published instance of standing trap 1 - adoption and activity metrics dressed as productivity - being falsified from inside the company that published the original number. Tier C rather than A because it is journalism on internal documents, not a filing; weighted heavily anyway because the direction of the reporting is against the company's own interest.

BMO Financial Group (Canadian Personal & Business Banking) Banking · Canada C4 — Non-tech · non-engineering

On the same Q3 2026 call (25 Aug 2026) CEO Darryl White said: "Our Lumi Assistant frontline chat box brings speed and efficiency to a new level, simplifying access to policy information across Canadian Personal & Business Banking, increasing productivity amongst new employees by 17%," and that Lumi "is being extended and scaled to support client conversations, starting with mortgage renewals." No dollar saving, no headcount action, no cost-per-contact figure and no expense line was attached. BMO reported record Q3 EPS of $3.96, up 22%, with no part of it attributed to Lumi.

Mechanism
A retrieval assistant over internal policy and procedure documents, used by branch and contact-centre staff so a new hire can answer a policy question without escalating to a tenured colleague. The cost path is ramp time for new frontline employees, not a reduction in existing headcount.
Why this shape
C4 - non-tech, non-engineering, the cell that matters, and a textbook instance of standing trap 1. `17%` productivity among *new employees only* is a ramp-time metric on the smallest and least expensive cohort of the frontline; it says nothing about steady-state cost and BMO did not convert it into one. The scope qualifier is doing heavy lifting and should not be dropped when this is quoted. Logged in the cost ledger and not as S1 because the mortgage-renewal extension is stated as a plan, not a result.
