Amazon Retail / Cloud · US S1 — Velocity⚠ Counter-evidence

On the Q2 2026 call (30 Jul 2026) CEO Andy Jassy said of Alexa for Shopping, Amazon's agentic shopping assistant, that "Over 350 million customers have used it in the last 12 months, and engagement accelerated in Q2, with active users nearly doubling and interactions up over 5x year-over-year", that in the U.S. "customers who use Alexa for Shopping spend an average of over 40% more per order than those who don't", and that "customers who've tried Alexa+ are signing up for Prime at nearly 25% higher rates". No incremental-sales dollar figure was given - two quarters after Amazon put a ~$12bn incremental annualized sales number on its AI shopping assistant.

Mechanism
An agentic shopping assistant giving personalised recommendations, product comparisons and price history, and automating purchase through price alerts and auto-buy, sitting in front of the retail catalogue; Alexa+ as the household entry point feeding Prime signup.
Why this shape
S1, not S2: the disclosed number is a cohort spend differential - an input/behavioural metric - not attributed incremental revenue. Selection bias is unaddressed and fatal to the naive reading, since engaged, high-frequency, high-spend shoppers are exactly the ones most likely to use an assistant; Amazon offers no counterfactual. Appendix scope because Amazon is a hyperscaler and out of scope as a subject in the main revenue log, but it belongs in the appendix as the sharpest available comparator: nobody in the main log has a consumer-facing AI cohort metric at this scale. The S1 standing caveat is the whole point of the entry - revenue conversion is conspicuously absent, and it was previously present. Per Modern Retail (Tier C, retrieved this run) Amazon disclosed at its February 2026 earnings that Rufus "was used by 300 million customers in 2025 and helped drive nearly $12 billion in incremental annualized sales", and Fortune (Nov 2025) quotes Jassy saying Rufus "is expected to generate over $10 billion in annual incremental sales for us". The word 'Rufus' does not appear anywhere in the Q2 2026 transcript and no dollar attribution replaces it. Confound stated plainly: no source retrieved this run confirms that Alexa for Shopping is a rebranded Rufus, so the metric basis may have changed rather than the disclosure being withdrawn.

Admiral Group Insurance · UK C3 — Non-tech · engineering

Admiral's H1 2026 results presentation (6 Aug 2026) discloses ">80% of code now generated with AI support in the UK" and "Further expansion of Predictive AI capability: 117 live models (+13% vs. YE); real-time models more than doubled". No cost, expense-ratio or revenue figure is attached to either. On the call the CEO called predictive AI "one of our biggest source of competitive advantage, with more models in production and better performance from them" and quantified only training inputs: "more than 10,000 hours of data and AI training in H1" and "a community of over 1,000 people".

Mechanism
AI coding assistance embedded across the software development lifecycle in UK operations; predictive models used in pricing, risk selection and claims; GenAI scaled into claims and customer communication including voice-to-voice, live in Admiral Money and Italy.
Why this shape
Cost ledger, C3: non-tech subject (a motor insurer), engineering function. Standing trap 1 in full force - '>80% of code generated with AI support' is an adoption metric, not a saving. It says nothing about cost per unit of output, headcount, or technology spend, and Admiral attaches no financial figure to it anywhere in the deck or on the call. Logged because it is a specific number from a Tier A primary document and because it is the highest AI-coding-adoption figure disclosed by a non-tech incumbent anywhere in this log - the comparators are Alphabet's 75% (C1, a tech subject) and BNY's share of engineers using the tools. METHOD's own warning applies: a coding gain on a small slice of an insurer's cost base is not a margin event, and H1 profit before tax fell 18% to GBP 429.2m on the UK motor cycle.

Lloyds Banking Group Banking · UK C4 — Non-tech · non-engineering

On the H1 2026 results and 'Accelerate 2030' strategy update call (30 Jul 2026) CEO Charlie Nunn said Lloyds expects "more than 100 million pounds of value from generative and agentic AI in 2026", and the CFO guided "around GBP 2 billion of gross cost savings from 2027-2030" from an AI-led efficiency programme. Nunn also said AI would deliver "substantial benefits driving our revenue growth and efficiency in Accelerate 2030" - with no revenue number attached to it. Lloyds separately reported ~800 live AI models and a plan to train 100% of colleagues in AI by end-2026.

Mechanism
Agentic AI agents deployed across customer service and internal operations; AI-powered credit decisions ('speed and quality'); automation of manual processes named as fraud and complaints; AI coding tools in engineering. The CFO frames the payoff as a "significant improvement in the number of active customers served per customer-facing colleague" - i.e. output per head in the branch/contact-centre estate, not in engineering, which is why this is C4 rather than C3.
Why this shape
Cost ledger, C4: non-tech subject (a UK retail and commercial bank) and the savings span customer operations, fraud, complaints and back office rather than software engineering. Standing trap 3 applies squarely to the GBP 100m: Lloyds calls it 'value' and does not say what it contains - cost avoided, loss avoided or revenue - and the same undefined construct was used for the ~GBP 50m claimed for 2025 (press release, 29 Jan 2026, retrieved this run). Trap 4-adjacent on the GBP 2bn: it is 'gross cost savings' for an entire four-year strategy in which AI is the headline lever but not the only one; RTE quotes Nunn as "technology such as AI", which is looser than the AI-only framing in the trade coverage. Logged once, in the cost ledger, because that is where the numbers sit; the revenue claim is qualitative.
