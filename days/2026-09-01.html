Shoprite Holdings Retail (grocery) · South Africa S1 — Velocity

On the FY2026 results call (1 Sep 2026) Shoprite disclosed that "98% of Xtra Savings Plus customers have used Pixie, who in the first 90 days, added more than 4 million products to their baskets", with users showing "larger baskets and faster shopping times" - no basket-size, order-value or sales figure attached to any of it.

Mechanism
Pixie is a generative-AI shopping assistant inside the Checkers Sixty60 delivery app. Its first feature, Smart Basket - CEO Pieter Engelbrecht's description was "Tinder for groceries" - predicts likely next purchases from loyalty and order history and lets the customer add them with a swipe; Pixie Smart Chat later added text, voice and image input. Launched April 2026 to Xtra Savings Plus subscribers, rolled out to all customers July 2026. The revenue path is more items per basket on a platform that did ZAR 25.5bn of sales in FY26, 11.1% of supermarkets RSA sales.
Why this shape
S1 because every disclosed number is an input metric - adoption (98% of a paid subscriber tier) and items added (4m+ in 90 days). "Larger baskets and faster shopping times" is directional with no magnitude, so no revenue is attributed and this is not S2. Revenue conversion is conspicuously absent in a specific way worth recording: Shoprite quantified Sixty60 sales growth of 34.7% to ZAR 25.5bn on the same call and did not attribute any part of it to Pixie, which reached all customers only in July, one month before year-end. Engelbrecht separately named a price optimisation tool as having "come in quite handy" in a low-inflation year and attached no number to that either.

NBER Working Paper 34984 - 'Artificial Intelligence, Productivity, and the Workforce: Evidence from Corporate Executives' (~750 executives) Cross-sector research · US C4 — Non-tech · non-engineering⚠ Counter-evidence

Survey of "nearly 750 corporate executives" documents "a productivity paradox, in which perceived productivity gains are larger than measured productivity gains, likely reflecting a delay in revenue realizations", and finds "little evidence of near-term aggregate employment declines due to AI". Issued March 2026 and surfaced unlogged on this run - six months old, flagged as such.

Mechanism
Executive survey run through the Atlanta Fed / Richmond Fed / Duke CFO network, asking firms directly about AI investment, realised labour-productivity effects and workforce composition. The load-bearing result for this log is the wedge between what executives believe AI has done for them and what shows up in measured output - the authors' own explanation for the wedge is that revenue has not arrived yet.
Why this shape
C4 by the precedent set for macro research entries (NBER 34836, BIS 1325, St. Louis Fed) - cross-sector, non-engineering, counted only in the cost ledger. Counter-evidence tagged against S2: it is a direct academic statement that the S1-to-S2 conversion this log keeps failing to observe is real and lagging, not merely under-disclosed. Trap 3 applies in reverse - the paper separates perceived from measured rather than pooling them into a composite value metric, which is why it is worth logging at all. Note the authors do report positive measured labour-productivity gains concentrated in high-skill services and finance; this is not a null result.
