UCSF Health Healthcare providers · US S2 — Share⚠ Counter-evidence

Peer-reviewed cohort study of 1,202,734 ambulatory encounters across 341,711 patients and 1,565 physicians at UCSF Health (698 ambient-AI-scribe adopters, 867 non-adopters), 1 Jan 2023 to 1 Apr 2025: adopters billed "1.81 (95% CI, 0.86-2.75) greater RVUs per week" - a 5.8% increase - and "0.80 (95% CI, 0.05-1.56) more encounters per week" - 2.8% - with no difference in claim denial rates, which the authors convert to "approximately $3,044 annually per physician" using the 2025 Medicare Physician Fee Schedule. The invited commentary in the same issue prices ambient scribes at "$200 to $600 per clinician per month", i.e. $2,400-$7,200 a year.

Mechanism
The scribe drafts the clinical note from the ambient consultation, cutting documentation and after-hours EHR time. The freed time converts two ways: slightly higher visit volume (+0.80 encounters/week) and slightly richer coding per encounter (+0.04 RVUs per encounter). Both bill through the existing fee schedule, so the revenue is realised at the physician-productivity line, not through any new service.
Why this shape
S2. (a) counterfactual purchase fails: the marginal patient would have been seen - later, or by another provider; (b) the prior alternative was an appointment elsewhere or a wait, not going without; (c) no ~24-month capability gate, ambient dictation with LLM note generation is the current generation but the service billed is unchanged; (d) nothing previously uneconomic is opened. Who lost the sale: whichever provider the deferred patient would otherwise have seen, and, for the coding-intensity half of the gain, the payer. Logged as counter-evidence because the direction of the magnitude matters more than its sign: $3,044 of attributed annual revenue per physician sits below the midpoint of the $2,400-$7,200 licence band the accompanying commentary cites, so the single best-evidenced revenue attribution in provider AI does not clearly clear its own subscription price on direct revenue alone. The commentary declines to call the ROI positive, arguing it must be read alongside "clinician well-being, organizational culture, operational efficiency" - which is the non-financial defence, not a financial one. Eight months old and unlogged; surfaced this run via 5 Aug 2026 trade coverage and flagged as stale rather than presented as new.

Riverside Health Healthcare providers · US S2 — Share

Riverside Health, a nine-hospital system in Newport News, Virginia, reported an "11% rise in physician relative value units (RVUs)" since deploying Abridge's ambient scribe in May 2024, with level-four encounters for returning patients up 4 percentage points and level-five encounters up 1 percentage point. SVP and Chief Medical Information Officer Charles Frazier said of the RVU gain: "That right there more than pays for what we're paying for [Abridge]" - and declined to specify what Riverside pays.

Mechanism
Same mechanism as the UCSF study but a larger claimed effect, and the disclosed detail points at where it comes from: the shift in the level-four and level-five encounter mix for returning patients. Fuller ambient documentation supports a higher evaluation-and-management code for the same visit. That is revenue captured per encounter, not output created.
Why this shape
S2, and an unusually literal one: the counterparty that loses is the payer, not a competing provider. On the four S3 tests it fails (a) and (b) outright - these are existing patients in existing visits. The entry is logged with its own objection attached: PwC's June 2026 projection has commercial healthcare cost trend rising "by up to 9% in 2027, with AI documentation and coding tools being one of the main contributors", and AHIP's Chris Bond calls the same mechanism "provider upcoding... adding billions of dollars in wasteful spending". If both readings are right, this is AI-enabled transfer between healthcare counterparties, which is the sharpest available example of METHOD's zero-sum warning - real firm-level ROI, GDP-neutral or worse. METHOD trap 5 also applies in spirit: Frazier's "more than pays for" is an unquantified assertion with the denominator withheld.

BCG CEO AI survey 2026 (152 CEOs, companies with revenue of at least $500m) Cross-sector research · Global S2 — Share⚠ Counter-evidence

BCG survey of "152 chief executives at companies with revenues of at least $500 million", released 22 Jul 2026: "Nearly nine in ten CEOs say their companies are now seeing cost or revenue benefits from AI in targeted areas", while "only 14%" have clearly defined the P&L impact for all AI initiatives and only 26% have embedded AI as part of a broader business transformation. More than half named linking AI initiatives to the P&L as a key barrier - a gap BCG itself scores at 42 percentage points.

Mechanism
None - this entry measures the absence of measurement. The load-bearing number is not the 88% who believe they see benefits, it is the 14% who have defined what the P&L impact of their AI initiatives actually is.
Why this shape
Counter-evidence tagged to S2, the shape that claims attributed financial impact on existing business. METHOD trap 3 applies directly and is why the headline cannot be used as revenue evidence: BCG pools "cost or revenue benefits" into a single construct and does not break the two apart anywhere in the release, so the 88% is uninterpretable for this log's question. The 14% is interpretable, and it says that for roughly six in seven large-cap CEOs claiming benefit, no defined P&L number exists behind the claim. Honest framing: this is corroboration, not news - it is the third independent reading in six weeks of the same gap, after the McKinsey State of AI 2026 wave (logged 2026-08-28, attribution flat year over year) and NBER WP 34984 (logged 2026-09-01, perceived productivity exceeding measured). It is also weaker than either, because unlike McKinsey's flat null the finding does not run against the surveyor's commercial interest: an execution gap is precisely what BCG sells the remedy for.

Deere & Company Industrials / Agricultural equipment · US S1 — Velocity

On the Q3 FY2026 call (20 Aug 2026) Deanna Kovar, President, Worldwide Agriculture & Turf Division, said "current EOP trends suggest factory adoption of See & Spray will nearly double, with the technology included on about one-third of North American sprayers on order", and that "for model year 2027, we are seeing more than 40% of North American Planters, including our next generation of advanced offerings such as ExactRate, ExactShot, and FurrowVision". No revenue, price premium or margin figure was attached to either. Production & Precision Ag net sales fell 6% in the same quarter.

Mechanism
See & Spray is camera-plus-machine-vision weed detection that fires individual nozzles on green plants rather than broadcasting herbicide. Deere sells it as a factory-installed option on sprayers, so the AI is inside the product and the revenue mechanism is option attach rate and price mix on equipment Deere already sells. The disclosed customer benefit is an input cost saving - "more than 50% herbicide savings" - which accrues to the farmer, not to Deere.
Why this shape
S1: the disclosed numbers are conversion/penetration metrics on the order book, not revenue. Revenue conversion is not merely absent, it is running the other way this quarter - Production & Precision Ag sales -6% - so the attach rate is currently mix inside a shrinking segment. Not S2, because Deere attributed no incremental revenue. Not S3 on all four tests: (a) a farmer buying a sprayer buys a sprayer regardless; (b) the prior alternative is a conventional broadcast sprayer sold by Deere or a competitor; (c) See & Spray has been commercially available since 2021, so it fails the ~24-month capability gate; (d) no previously uneconomic segment is opened - the same farms are served at a better herbicide cost. Separately noted: Kovar said Deere "will soon build on that foundation with AI-enabled capabilities" inside Operations Center - future tense, no product, no revenue, and therefore not a finding.
