Cross-run pattern, now the strongest signal in this log: the conversion step is where AI revenue claims die, and companies are migrating their own metrics from revenue to cost rather than the other way round. Customers Bancorp did it inside one quarter. Travelers states the sequence as policy. SPI measures it across 245 firms at nearly 3:1. Wyndham's headline degraded from franchisee dollars to "opportunities". Four independent sources, one mechanism.
Second pattern: where a conversion number does exist, nobody controls for selection. Neptune's 3.5x and Customers Bancorp's 100% prospecting lift are both comparisons between users and non-users of a tool people opted into. Not one disclosure retrieved in 62 logged days has offered a holdout.

The Travelers Companies, Inc. Insurance · US S2 — Share⚠ Counter-evidence

On the Q3 2026 earnings call (16 Oct 2026) Chairman and CEO Alan Schnitzer set out the AI payoff sequence for the largest US commercial P&C writer and put revenue last: early benefits show up in expenses and the loss ratio, so "we'll see it in margins, and over time, that's going to translate into growth". Asked by Josh Shanker (Bank of America) whether AI and technology savings would be passed to customers, he cited lower software development, first-notice-of-loss labour and policy-servicing costs as operating leverage that Travelers could "redeploy some of those into pricing without compromising our return objectives". He also said it is hard to draw a straight line from better underwriting insights to specific results and that some benefits cannot be quantified. On AI in claim handling: "AI gives our claim professionals a faster, deeper command of claim files" and "we are using AI to sharpen our responses to their demands". Net written premium was a Q3 record at $5.9bn and retention "remained very strong at 86%"; neither figure is attributed to AI.

Mechanism
AI in claim-file comprehension and litigated-demand response, plus lower software-development and policy-servicing unit costs. The stated route to revenue is indirect: bank the cost saving, then optionally spend it on price to win share from carriers that cannot.
Why this shape
Tagged S2 because the only revenue mechanism management describes is price-funded share capture - cheaper operations let Travelers underprice rivals on the same risks in the same segments, which is a competitor's lost premium, zero-sum at industry level and GDP-neutral. It is counter-evidence because the CEO declines to claim any of it yet: margins first, growth "over time", and an explicit admission that the underwriting-insight benefit cannot be drawn in a straight line or quantified. Note Schnitzer's separate "Travelers will be a consolidator of premium" remark was about scale generally and he did not tie it to AI. No AI-attributed premium, revenue or hit-rate figure was given anywhere on the call.

SPI Research - '2026 Impact of AI on Professional Services' (245 organisations: 175 independent professional-services firms, 70 embedded services organisations; fielded 15 Jul - 16 Sep 2026) Professional services · Global S2 — Share⚠ Counter-evidence

Benchmark study released 20 Oct 2026 that measures the cost-versus-revenue asymmetry this log exists to test. AI is now used on "43.6% of professional services projects", up from 23.1% in 2025, and 86.6% of firms say AI has "increased productivity and efficiency", up from 71.5%. But while 84.8% of organisations report "realizing value through time savings", only 29.9% report value through "revenue generation" - and 36.8% of firms "do not formally track any AI outcomes" at all. Research director R. David Hofferberth: "Saving time isn't the same as increasing revenue." Operational spread: firms using AI on more than 80% of projects report "90.7% on-time delivery" versus 76.3% for firms under 20%; firms running complex agents or AI-first models report "professional services EBITDA margins of 20.5%" versus 13.3% for chat-assisted or no formal AI use.

Mechanism
Not a deployment - a cross-sectional measurement of where deploying firms say the value lands. The release names the trap directly: hourly-billing firms risk passing time savings to clients as lower billed hours rather than capturing additional revenue, which is the structural reason a productivity gain in a billable-hours business can be revenue-negative.
Why this shape
Tagged to S2 as the shape it contradicts: the near-3:1 gap between firms realising time savings (84.8%) and firms realising revenue (29.9%) is direct evidence against the claim that AI productivity converts into top-line, in the one sector where the conversion should be most mechanical because the input saved is the thing being sold. Deliberately not logged in the cost ledger: the 20.5%-vs-13.3% EBITDA spread is a cross-sectional correlation between self-selected maturity tiers, not an attributed saving, and more mature firms are plausibly better-run for reasons unrelated to AI - so the loggable content here is the revenue-side absence, not a cost result. The 36.8% who track nothing is the figure to keep: a third of this market cannot answer the ROI question either way.

Wyndham Hotels & Resorts Hospitality · US S2 — Share⚠ Counter-evidence

Third quarter at the most AI-vocal hotel franchisor, and the falsifier written here on 2026-08-02 held. On the Q3 2026 call (22 Oct 2026) CEO Geoff Ballotti said Wyndham Connect, now at more than 5,100 hotels, "has generated tens of millions of dollars in year-to-date upsell opportunities for participating franchisees" - opportunities, not booked revenue. Wyndham AI Concierge is live at more than 1,700 hotels, up 17% sequentially, "answering tens of thousands of previously missed calls"; the AI agents "answered over 3 million calls year-to-date" against an average of "over 25%" of inbound calls going unanswered at franchised hotels. The Q2 figures are repeated unchanged - reservation requests converting at an ADR 15% above hotel-booked rates, "over 500 basis points of increased direct contribution for franchisees", and per-franchisee uplift of $100,000-$300,000 a year at upscale properties or roughly $500-$2,000 a month at small ones. Corporate Q3 net revenue was $410m, up 7%, with US RevPAR up 6%; none of it is attributed to AI, and the words AI, Wyndham Connect and AI Concierge do not appear anywhere in the Q3 earnings release. On autonomous AI booking traffic Ballotti said "it is extremely early" and "the actual volume of observable activity is pretty minor"; on distribution costs he said GEO "may well prove" cheaper than SEO, "that to be determined".

Mechanism
AI voice and messaging agents answer inbound calls franchised front desks were dropping, convert them into direct bookings at a higher ADR than the hotel's own booked rate, and upsell at check-in - shifting share from OTAs and from competitor hotels that capture the abandoned call.
Why this shape
S2, and firmly so: a guest who could not get through to a Wyndham property booked somewhere, almost certainly an OTA or a competing hotel, so the counterfactual purchase existed and someone else lost it. Recapture of abandoned demand is share, not new TAM. Counter-evidence because the falsifier logged on 2026-08-02 specified that repeating the per-franchisee dollars and the 15% ADR premium into Q3/Q4 without converting them into disclosed corporate fee revenue, a RevPAR index gain or a direct-channel mix shift would mark the claim as narrative - and that is precisely what the quarter delivered, with the new headline number deteriorating from dollars to "upsell opportunities". The written release's complete silence on AI, while the call narrates it at length, is the tell: these numbers live where management is not on the hook for them.

Mechanism

Mechanism

Why this shape

Logged separately from the S1 entry because this is a distinct statement with its own number and its own footnote, not the same claim counted twice - the S1 entry is about the revenue claim that vanished.
