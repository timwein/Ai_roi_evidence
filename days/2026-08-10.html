Booking Holdings Travel / Hospitality · US S2 — Share⚠ Counter-evidence

On the Q2 2026 call (4 Aug 2026) CFO Ewout Steenbergen said "traffic from large language models remains below 1% of room nights," adding it "hasn't moved so much recently" and represents "no material change over the last few months or quarters." Asked to quantify conversion or satisfaction gains from Priceline's agentic assistant Penny, CEO Glenn Fogel declined: "it's still very small, and we're not at a stage we're going to start giving away percentages." The only quantified AI benefit disclosed was cost - customer service cost per booking falling at a double-digit rate - against AI expense running at "a low single-digit level of our overall technology spend."

Mechanism
None - this is the absence of a mechanism. The claimed path (guests discover and book travel inside an LLM, so whoever wins the agentic channel wins the demand) is measured here at the point where it would show up in the P&L, room nights, and it is under 1% and flat.
Why this shape
Counter-evidence tagged to S2. It contradicts the channel-capture thesis logged for Wyndham on 2026-08-02, where CEO Geoff Ballotti said "roughly 60% of travel searches by our guests are occurring within an LLM" with "a 20% higher conversion on our brand.com sites when the guest connects to us from an LLM." These are not formally the same metric - searches by one franchisor's guests is not room nights across the world's largest OTA - but they are the same claim measured at two points, and the harder number is the small one. The mismatch is the finding: the LLM channel is loud upstream in search and negligible downstream in bookings. Note also that Booking is the incumbent with the most to lose from agentic disintermediation and therefore an incentive to describe the channel as small; that cuts against the disclosure, and is why the flat trend statement matters more than the level.

Skyward Specialty Insurance Group Insurance · US S1 — Velocity

On the Q2 2026 call (5 Aug 2026) CEO Andrew Robinson described "bionic underwriting" - "this idea of being able to automate the ingestion of everything that is submission related" - and disclosed "40% faster submissions to underwriters, 35% improvement in speed to quote." He added that "half of our underwriting is benefiting from machine learning and predictive analytics," and described SkyScore, which "allows us to ingest all financial information without any human intervention to score every principal on 10 different dimensions" in Surety.

Mechanism
Submission intake in E&S is the binding constraint on premium: an underwriter can only quote what reaches the desk in a usable form. Automating ingestion of broker submissions raises the number of risks an underwriter sees per day and shortens time-to-quote, which in a broker-intermediated market raises the probability of being the first quote back and therefore the hit ratio. SkyScore extends the same logic into Surety by machine-scoring the principal's financials rather than queueing them for a human credit analyst.
Why this shape
S1: every disclosed number is an input metric - submission speed, speed to quote, share of underwriting touched by ML. No premium dollar is attributed to any of it. Revenue conversion is CONSPICUOUSLY ABSENT and the absence is unusually clean: gross written premium grew 13% year over year on the same call and management attributed that growth to business mix and market diversification (A&H, Agriculture, Credit & Surety) and to underwriting discipline - not to AI. The only financial line management did connect to AI was the expense ratio, which improved 2.7 points to 24.3%. Per METHOD's one-disclosure-one-ledger rule this is logged once, in the revenue ledger, because the 40%/35% throughput figures are what the entry is about; the expense-ratio reading is noted here rather than double-logged as C4.

Zillow Group Real estate · US S1 — Velocity

On the Q2 2026 call (5 Aug 2026) CEO Jeremy Wacksman said: "Consumers who use AI mode spend more than three times as long on Zillow, view more than twice as many homes, run nearly three times as many searches - and contact an agent at nearly three times the rate of consumers who don't use AI mode." AI mode is live for roughly 20% of signed-in users. Q2 revenue was $772m, up 18%, and none of it was attributed to AI mode.

Mechanism
Agent contacts are the unit Zillow sells: Premier Agent revenue is a function of connection volume and price per connection. A conversational search mode that triples the contact rate per user is, mechanically, a Premier Agent inventory expansion - if the effect is causal.
Why this shape
S1: every number is an engagement input, none is revenue. Revenue conversion is conspicuously absent - 18% revenue growth was not tied to AI mode, and no connection-volume dollar impact was given. The severe caveat, which management did not address: this is a self-selection statistic. The consumer who opts into AI mode is the high-intent consumer who was always going to contact an agent. No holdout, no control, no causal claim was offered. Logged at low confidence for exactly that reason, and it is the cleanest current example of the AI engagement metric that looks like a revenue mechanism and may be a sorting artefact.
