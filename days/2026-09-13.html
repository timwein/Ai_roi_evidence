People Incorporated (formerly IAC; owner of People Inc. Group, formerly Dotdash Meredith) Media / Digital publishing · US S2 — Share⚠ Counter-evidence

In a recast Management's Discussion and Analysis filed as Exhibit 99.1 to a Form 8-K on 11 Sep 2026, People Incorporated states that fiscal 2025 digital Advertising revenue fell $5.4 million, or 1%, and that "the programmatic revenue decline was due primarily to lower impression volumes driven by a 5% decline in Core Sessions, due primarily to the impact of the increasing prominence of Google AI Overviews on Google search sessions." The filing adds, on the record and forward-looking as of 11 Sep 2026: "The Company expects the increasing prominence of Google AI Overviews to continue to negatively impact Core Sessions and advertising revenue." The same document reports Licensing and Other revenue up $32.9 million, or 28%, "due primarily to improved performance of Apple News+ and content syndication partners and to the contribution of a full year of OpenAI revenue, a partnership which began in May 2024."

Mechanism
Google's AI Overviews answers the query on the search results page, so the click that used to land on a People Inc. property never happens. Fewer sessions means fewer ad impressions, and programmatic revenue is impression-volume times rate. Higher programmatic rates and premium direct sales offset part of it; the volume loss is the AI effect. Running the other way in the same P&L, AI labs became content buyers - the OpenAI partnership sits inside Licensing and Other revenue.
Why this shape
S2, tagged as counter-evidence because it is the share shape running in reverse at the incumbent. The advertiser still spends and the query still gets answered; what moved is who captures the impression - from the publisher to the platform's own answer surface. Zero-sum at the industry level and GDP-neutral, which is exactly the S2 test, with People Inc. as the party that lost the sale and Google as the party that took it. The OpenAI licensing line is the S3-shaped offset in the same filing and is deliberately NOT logged as S3: the $32.9 million increase is jointly attributed to Apple News+, content syndication and OpenAI, so no AI-attributed figure can be isolated and test (a) cannot be scored on a number the company did not break out.

Rent the Runway, Inc. Retail / Apparel rental · US S1 — Velocity

In its Q2 fiscal 2026 results release, filed as an SEC Form 8-K exhibit on 11 Sep 2026, Rent the Runway states that it "completed the rollout of AI-powered outfits generation to all customers" and reports the pilot read: "Engagement with the feature in our app is running approximately 35% and during the pilot, customers with the experience added items to their bag approximately 12% more often than those without it." In the same release total revenue was $97.7 million, up 20.8% year over year, and add-on bookings were up 81%, with that growth attributed to "higher subscriber engagement with our assortment and membership flexibility" - not to AI.

Mechanism
A generative outfitting layer on the rental catalogue assembles complete looks rather than returning individual garments, so a subscriber browsing a single dress is shown a wearable combination. The claimed effect runs through discovery: more items enter the bag per session because the customer is shown what goes together instead of having to imagine it. Avatars were added to the same surface in August 2026 and virtual try-on is in pilot.
Why this shape
S1, because the disclosed number is an input metric - add-to-bag frequency and feature engagement - not revenue. The methodology is unusually clean for this log: a with-versus-without comparison during a pilot, which is closer to a controlled read than the self-selected adopter cohorts most retailers report. But bag adds are two conversions removed from rented units and three from revenue. Revenue conversion is conspicuously absent: the same release attributes 20.8% revenue growth and 81% add-on bookings growth to assortment and membership flexibility and puts no dollar, unit or AOV figure next to AI.

Orion180 Insurance Group Inc. Insurance / Personal lines property · US S1 — Velocity

In an amended Form S-1 registration statement filed 9 Sep 2026, Orion180 states: "The use of automation and machine learning models in our underwriting process enables significant quote and bind throughput as we generated approximately 3,700,000 new quotes, bound approximately 397,000 new policies, and processed approximately 28,000 claims from the year ended December 31, 2021 through the six months ended June 30, 2026", and that its underwriting engine's "use of machine learning algorithms allow us to provide bindable quotes without the need for human interaction upfront." On risk selection it claims "an average direct loss ratio of 36% since inception" and that "since 2018, we outperform external industry models for catastrophe events by more than two times, with $76 million of aggregate modeled losses as compared to $33 million of actual losses incurred." Consolidated total revenues for the six months ended 30 Jun 2026 were $80.1 million against $50.4 million, up 58.8%, with net income of $13.5 million against a $3.0 million loss. In the Underwriting segment, total revenue was $45.6 million against $32.7 million, up 39.5% - attributed in the filing to "gross premiums written growth", not to the models - and the segment combined ratio was 124.7% against 158.4%.

Mechanism
The MY180 platform pulls 47 property and 11 personal data fields from 12 third-party data providers, plus aerial imagery and tech-enabled self-inspections, and runs proprietary ML models to predict claims frequency and severity, retention propensity and property-specific flood load factors. The output is a bindable quote produced without human review, which is what converts a data pipeline into throughput: quotes issued per unit of underwriter time rises, and human underwriters are redeployed onto portfolio-level exposure management.
Why this shape
S1: the numbers are input metrics - quotes generated, policies bound, straight-through binding without human touch. Not S3. The company writes ordinary homeowners cover in catastrophe-exposed states; the filing contains no use of the word "uninsurable", and a residual or surplus-lines market already serves these buyers, so test (b) fails outright - absent Orion180 these homeowners buy from a competitor or the state pool. Scope is appendix on the Neptune precedent (logged 28 Jul 2026): this is a platform-built insurance entrant with "over 40% of our workforce (including the third-party developers that we employ) dedicated to IT and data science", not an incumbent deploying AI onto an existing book. Revenue conversion is disclosed but not attributed: revenue is up 58.8% and the filing credits premium growth, not the models.
