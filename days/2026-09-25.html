FRBSF Economic Letter 2026-25 - 'How AI Adoption Might Affect Bank Lending' (US bank Call Report panel, 2021-2025) Banking (research) · US S3 — New TAM⚠ Counter-evidence

Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco research published 21 Sep 2026 (Avaradi, Halbersleben, Liu, Spiegel) ranks US banks by the AI-related share of their job postings and compares outcomes: "The average ROA for high-AI banks is about 0.38 percentage point higher than for low-AI banks", but "In our full sample, high-AI banks have a much smaller average share of SME loans than low-AI banks (12% versus 21%)", and "High-AI banks also have higher average shares of problem loans than low-AI banks, although the disparity is small." The authors state the results "support the hypothesis that increased AI adoption is associated with more risk-taking in bank lending."

Mechanism
AI intensity is proxied by the AI-related share of a bank's job postings (banking sector-wide that share went from "less than 0.94% in 2015" to "6.80% by the end of 2025"; 8.86% at large banks versus 1.15% at small banks). Outcomes come from FFIEC Call Report regulatory filings, 2021-2025, using the Call Report definition of SME loans - commercial loans under $1m and farm loans under $500,000. The economic channel the authors propose is that AI is good at processing hard, codified data and therefore pushes banks toward larger, more complex credits and away from soft-information relationship lending.
Why this shape
Counter-evidence tagged to S3, and tagged there deliberately. METHOD names "long-tail or SMB segments served at quality levels that previously required enterprise pricing" as one of the most productive hunting grounds for genuine new TAM, and the standard bull case for AI in lending is exactly that: cheaper underwriting opens credit to borrowers the cost curve previously excluded. This is the first identified panel evidence this log has found that runs the other way. The banks that adopted AI hardest did not expand into the small-business long tail - their SME share is roughly nine percentage points, or about 43%, below low-AI banks. Read against the four S3 tests, it attacks test (d) directly: AI moved the cost curve, and the banks spent the gain going up-market rather than down. Two limits stated plainly. First, the authors say "these results are correlations and do not necessarily reflect causal effects", and the composition story is at least as plausible as the behavioural one - large banks post more AI jobs and have always done less SME lending. Second, the ROA gain is real and is a revenue-side result at the firm level, so this entry is not one-directional: it is S2-flavoured evidence for the firm and S3-negative evidence for the economy, which is precisely the distinction this log exists to keep apart.

Falsifier

Confirmed and strengthened if the 2026-2027 Call Report panel shows the SME share gap widening while the ROA gap persists.
