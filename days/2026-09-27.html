Blue Cross Blue Shield Association - 'Rising Coding Intensity and Its Impact on Health Care Affordability' (commercial inpatient claims, BCBSA member plans) Healthcare - hospitals / payers · US S2 — Share

A BCBSA claims analysis published 24 Sep 2026 estimates that hospitals' use of AI coding and ambient-documentation tools added `$942m` to BCBSA member plans' inpatient spend over 2024-2025, of which `$653m` came from secondary diagnoses. More than `55,000` hospital stays were moved into higher-paying diagnosis-related groups at just under `$11,900` a case. The share of admissions billed as medically complex rose from `37%` at the start of 2023 to `40%` at the end of 2025; for bowel procedures the complex share went from `10.2%` to `22.7%`. BCBSA's stated finding is a sharp increase in patients documented as having complex conditions with "no evidence of corresponding change in care delivered." Senior VP of product and data science Luke Chalker: "The disconnect between diagnoses and treatment suggests that AI is identifying more billable conditions, not sicker patients."

Mechanism
Inpatient reimbursement is tiered: the same procedure pays at one of up to three DRG severity levels, and a single qualifying secondary diagnosis - a complication/comorbidity (CC) or major CC - moves a claim up a step worth `42%` to `173%` more. AI documentation and coding tools read the entire chart and surface codeable comorbidities a human coder misses or skips. The admission, the procedure and the treatment are unchanged; the code set and therefore the bill move up. Per BCBSA, about `70%` of the coding-intensity increase comes from those secondary-diagnosis reclassifications.
Why this shape
S2, and the least flattering example of it in this log. The counterfactual is explicit and the source measured it: the payer would have paid for the same admission at a lower tier, so the incremental revenue is real for the provider and zero-sum at industry level. The unusual feature is who loses - not a competitor, but the customer. Not S3 on any of the four tests: no buyer newly entered the market (a), the prior alternative was the same admission billed lower (b), the capability gate is arguable but irrelevant since no new demand exists (c), and nothing previously uneconomic became serveable (d). Not S1: the disclosed number is dollars, not a process metric. Recorded weakness: BCBSA does not observe AI tool use directly - it infers it from coding-intensity drift - and the American Hospital Association counters with a Vizient analysis showing hospital case-mix index up roughly `5%` from 2019 to 2024 on genuine ageing and chronic-disease acuity.

Mechanism

No decomposition into cost avoidance, realized expense reduction, loss avoidance or revenue is offered, and no baseline or denominator is given against a cost base of tens of billions.
