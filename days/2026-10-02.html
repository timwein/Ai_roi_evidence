Concentrix Corporation Business services - customer experience outsourcing · US S2 — Share⚠ Counter-evidence

On the Q3 FY2026 call and in the same-day earnings release (both 29 Sep 2026) Concentrix put the largest AI revenue figure of the quarter on the record and it is a denominator construct. CEO Chris Caldwell sized roughly `$5bn` of "AI-influenced" revenue in three parts: "$3 billion of revenue...from new and existing clients that have either gone through heavy transformation or has AI influencing the revenue; $1.3 billion of net revenue resulting from the compression of our traditional services using our iX Suite AI platform; and finally, $700 million of revenue from new high-value services." The release adds: "50% of our revenue is coming from business we have won and deployed within the last 3 years since the introduction of AI." Against that, Q3 revenue was `$2,453.7m`, down `1.2%` year on year as reported and `0.5%` in constant currency; the quarter carried a non-cash goodwill impairment of `$1,050.0m` "primarily resulting from the trading range for the Company's stock price and market capitalization"; Q4 guidance is a constant-currency decline of `3.0%` to `5.0%` and full-year FY2026 guidance is `$9.827bn` to `$9.877bn`, a constant-currency decline of `0.3%` to `0.8%`. The one genuinely new SKU is small and forward-dated: "We expect our software licensing Annualized Recurring Revenue (ARR) of approximately $120 million by the end of fiscal 2026" - about `1.2%` of guided revenue.

Mechanism
Three mechanisms bundled under one number, only one of which is a product. (1) Client relationships where "AI is influencing the revenue" - a relationship attribute, not a transaction. (2) `$1.3bn` of "net revenue resulting from the compression of our traditional services" using the iX Suite platform, i.e. revenue that survived the automation of work Concentrix used to bill by the hour - retained revenue counted as AI revenue. (3) `$700m` of new high-value services (risk and compliance), plus an expected `$120m` of software licensing ARR from licensing iX Suite directly. Caldwell also disclosed "New logo AI sales...up 63% quarter over quarter" and a revenue retention rate on new services "4x higher" than the traditional business.
Why this shape
Appendix, not main. Concentrix is a services firm selling AI transformation and now licensing its own AI platform, so this `$5bn` describes its own sales of AI rather than ROI on AI it deployed in a non-tech operating business - the shovel-seller exclusion in METHOD.md, applied the same way as the Infosys entry of 2026-07-29. Logged as counter-evidence against S2 because it is the cleanest specimen this log has of two standing skepticism rules firing at once. Denominator games: "AI-influenced" and "50% of revenue won since the introduction of AI" are constructs with no counterfactual and no falsifiable boundary - a client relationship can be AI-influenced without a dollar of incremental spend, and revenue won in the last three years would be a large share of any outsourcer's book whether or not AI existed. Attribution laundering: the `$1.3bn` leg counts revenue that *did not leave* as revenue AI produced. The arithmetic is the tell - roughly `$5bn` of AI-influenced revenue, about half the company, sits inside a top line that is shrinking in constant currency with a `$1.05bn` goodwill write-off taken in the same quarter. Only the `$120m` software ARR leg has the shape of a new product with no predecessor in the catalogue, it is a forward expectation rather than a result, and at about `1.2%` of guided revenue it does not offset a declining base. Not S3: buyers of CX software previously bought it from Genesys, NICE or Verint, so test (b) fails and a competitor loses the sale.
