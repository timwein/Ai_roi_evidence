Direct test of the falsifier written into this log's 2026-09-22 Costco entry. On the Q4 FY2026 call (24 Sep 2026) CFO Gary Millerchip kept the AI channel in prepared remarks - "Traffic to our site from AI search grew triple digits for the second consecutive quarter", again with the highest conversion rate of any traffic source, "AI continues to grow in its influence on how our members are searching for products", and "sales originated from AI search are led by appliances and consumer electronics" - but still put no dollars and no basis points of digital sales against it. The `$0.5bn` of personalised-recommendation-carousel sales disclosed for Q3 was NOT repeated: personalisation is now described as "triple digit growth in sales from personalized initiatives in Q4" with "10% of all costco.com orders now include a personalized item". Digitally enabled comps were `19.5%` (`19.8%` ex-FX) and full-year digital sales above `$33bn`, up more than `20%`, none of it AI-attributed.

Mechanism
Unchanged from Q3 and now one quarter older: Costco rewrites product pages so large language models retrieve and cite them, turning answer engines into an unpaid acquisition channel - Costco buys no digital advertising, so LLM search is a channel where its price position shows up without a media budget. The second, larger mechanism is on-site personalisation - recommendation carousels, product placements and email - which management does not label AI.
Why this shape
S1 on the same test as the Q3 entry: every number attached to AI is an input metric (traffic growth, conversion ranking, category mix), with revenue conversion conspicuously absent for a second consecutive quarter. Logged as counter-evidence because the specific tell written into the 2026-09-22 falsifier fired: the one dollar figure in the personalisation story was dropped and replaced with an unsized growth rate plus an order-penetration percentage. Disclosure moved from a quantity that can be tracked (`$470m`, then just under `$500m`) to two that cannot be trended against anything. The new `10%` of orders containing a personalised item is a penetration metric, not revenue. Note the direction of the remaining evidence: two consecutive quarters where AI-search traffic converts better than every other channel and is still too small to size is a channel that is real and immaterial at the same time.

Societe Generale Banking · France C4 — Non-tech · non-engineering⚠ Counter-evidence

Widely reported this week as an AI cost number; the primary document does not say that. Societe Generale's strategic roadmap press release of 21 Sep 2026 states, under the heading "Further reduce costs": "Our fundamental work in terms of simplification, organization and process re-engineering will gradually be supported by controlled developments in AI. The implementation of numerous initiatives at Group level will generate between EUR 500 million and EUR 600 million in savings, including approximately EUR 350 million already identified by 2029." The savings are attributed to simplification and process re-engineering, which AI will "gradually" support - not to AI. Separately the plan targets "a decrease in IT spending of approximately EUR -0.5 billion, bringing our IT intensity ratio to 12% by 2029, thanks to further simplifications, elimination of redundancies and obsolete solutions, and productivity gains enabled by AI", where AI is the last of four named drivers. The release also announces "a strategic agreement with Anthropic". No revenue is attributed to AI anywhere in the plan.

Mechanism
Group-level process re-engineering and organisational simplification through 2029, with AI positioned as an accelerant rather than the cause. On the IT line the mechanism is stated more concretely: retiring redundant and obsolete systems plus AI-enabled developer and operations productivity, taking IT intensity from about 15% of revenue to 12%.
Why this shape
C4, non-tech / non-engineering, and logged as counter-evidence to the cost thesis rather than support for it. Trap 4 (headcount and cost actions with many parents) in its purest published form: the company names three causes and puts AI third as a gradual support, and the figure still travelled as "SocGen sees AI cutting up to EUR 600 million in costs". Also trap 5 - EUR 350 million is "already identified", which is a plan line, not an expense that has fallen. The IT-spend target has a partial C3 reading, since developer productivity is one of four drivers, but it is logged once here because the EUR 500-600 million group figure is what the entry is about. The value of this entry is the gap between the primary and its coverage: if this log had taken the headline, it would have recorded an AI cost saving the bank never claimed.
