C3.ai, Inc. Software / Enterprise AI applications · US S2 — Share⚠ Counter-evidence

In its Q1 FY2027 results, filed as an SEC Form 8-K exhibit on 2 Sep 2026 for the quarter ended 31 Jul 2026, C3.ai reported total revenue of $52.4 million against $70.3 million in the prior-year quarter - down 25.5% - and subscription revenue of $49.2 million against $60.3 million, down 18.4%. Full-year FY2027 guidance is $210.0m-$240.0m, against FY2026 actual revenue of $250.3 million (itself down from $389.1 million in FY2025, per the 3 Jun 2026 release). The FY2027 guidance midpoint of $225m implies a further ~10% decline, a third consecutive year of contraction. On the same-day call CFO Hitesh Lath said 'Total revenue for the quarter was $52.4 million. Subscription revenue was $49.2 million, representing 94% of total revenue.' CEO Thomas Siebel's release quote addresses the trajectory without disputing the direction: 'Revenue has stabilized, free cash flow is positive, operating loss has narrowed, and Forrester Research named C3 AI a leader in Enterprise AI.'

Mechanism
C3.ai is the only US-listed pure-play vendor of enterprise AI applications sold predominantly into non-tech buyers - utilities, oil and gas, manufacturing, defence, financial services. Its revenue line is therefore an unusually direct read on the other side of this log's question: not what incumbents say about AI, but what they actually pay third parties for AI applications. Named Q1 customers include Ford and Johnson & Johnson. Where every other data point in this log is a deploying company's self-report, this one is a cash-settled measurement of demand.
Why this shape
Appendix, because the subject is a software vendor and out of scope for the revenue log as a deployer. Tagged S2 because the claim it contradicts is the S2 thesis - that non-tech incumbents are buying AI capability and converting it into attributed revenue. Marked counter_evidence: if non-tech incumbents were converting AI budgets into production deployments at scale, the one public company whose entire P&L depends on selling them exactly that would not be in its third year of revenue contraction. The confounders are real and must be stated rather than buried: a ~40% headcount reduction, a leadership change and return of the founder-CEO, the lapse of the Baker Hughes partnership, and a deliberate pivot from selling applications to selling the C3 Code platform all sit inside this number. Forward indicators point the other way - federal bookings up 138% year over year per Siebel on the call, 22 enterprise agreements closed, free cash flow positive at $2.1m against negative $34.3m a year earlier. So this is evidence about one vendor's execution as much as about market demand, and the honest reading is that it fails to show the demand signal the capex narrative requires, not that it proves demand is absent.
