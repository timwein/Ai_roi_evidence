Concentrix Corporation Business services / BPO · US S2 — Share⚠ Counter-evidence

On its Q3 FY2026 results (quarter ended 31 Aug 2026, reported 29 Sep 2026) Concentrix told investors that roughly `50%` of revenue now comes from business won and deployed since AI was introduced at the start of fiscal 2023, and broke out about `$5bn` of "new, AI-influenced revenue": `$3bn` from clients whose programmes have been heavily transformed or are AI-influenced, `$1.3bn` of net revenue tied to traditional services compressed through its proprietary iX Suite AI platform, and `$700m` from newer high-value services such as risk and compliance. Management said this stream is growing at an estimated `30%` year over year in fiscal 2026 with revenue retention "more than four times higher" than traditional business, that new-logo AI wins rose `63%` quarter over quarter, and that the company expects to exit fiscal 2026 with approximately `$120m` of software-licensing ARR from iX Suite. Consolidated Q3 revenue was `$2,453.7m`, down `1.2%` reported and `0.5%` in constant currency year over year, and Q4 constant-currency revenue is guided down `3%` to `5%`.

Mechanism
Two mechanisms sit inside one label. Concentrix uses its iX Suite to compress delivered CX work - fewer advisor-hours per programme, `61` opportunities brought live on iX Suite in the quarter across more than `30,000` advisors - and separately licenses that same AI stack back to clients as software. The first shrinks the billable base of the legacy business; the second is a new licence line. "AI-influenced revenue" pools both with any account won since FY2023, so it measures mix, not increment.
Why this shape
S2, not S3. Test (a) fails: buyers of outsourced CX and of CX AI software would have bought from Teleperformance, TTEC, Genpact, NICE or Genesys absent Concentrix, so a competitor loses the sale and nothing here is new demand. The `$120m` software-licensing ARR is the one item with no predecessor in Concentrix's own catalogue, but it is roughly `1.2%` of FY2026 guided revenue and is PROJECTED at year-end exit rather than realised, so it does not carry an S3-CANDIDATE on its own. Logged as counter-evidence because this is the cleanest live example in the log of the denominator game METHOD warns about: `$5bn` of revenue relabelled AI-influenced while the consolidated top line falls `1.2%` and the next quarter is guided down `3-5%` in constant currency. Note also the direction of the compression mechanism - iX Suite deflates the revenue of the work it touches, so at a services firm a genuine AI success can show up as a smaller top line.

Albert Heijn (Ahold Delhaize) Retail / grocery · Netherlands S2 — Share

At Groceryshop 2026 in Las Vegas (22-24 Sep 2026) Albert Heijn said its in-app AI assistant "Steijn" has `1.1 million` active users, and that those users spend `56%` more than non-users. The figure was reported independently by three trade outlets covering the same sessions.

Mechanism
Steijn sits inside the My AH app with access to more than `20,000` Albert Heijn recipes plus nutrition and cooking data, and is being extended from meal inspiration into personalised meal planning, pre-filled baskets and coupon activation. The revenue path is conversion of recipe browsing - previously a dead end that ended in inspiration - into a multi-item basket placed in the same session.
Why this shape
S2 at most, and possibly not even that. Test (a) fails for S3 outright: these are existing Albert Heijn customers buying groceries they would have bought from somebody. The deeper problem is that the `56%` is a cross-sectional comparison of users against non-users with no control for shopper type - households that plan meals, cook for families and shop heavily are exactly the ones who adopt a meal-planning assistant, so the gap may be selection rather than effect. Who lost the sale is unidentified, and it is entirely possible that nobody did and this is Albert Heijn's own heavy shoppers sorted into a cohort. That is the reason this is logged at low confidence rather than as a clean share finding.

UK Department for Science, Innovation and Technology - 'AI Adoption Research' (IFF Research / Technopolis Group; 3,500 UK businesses) Cross-sector (UK business population) · UK S2 — Share⚠ Counter-evidence

UK government-commissioned research published `13 Feb 2026`, surveying `3,500` businesses with `100` qualitative interviews: among businesses using AI, `77%` reported having not yet seen a change in revenue and just `12%` reported an increase. Over the same base `56%` reported an increase in employee productivity (`24%` by less than `10%`, `16%` by `10-19%`, `16%` by `20%` or more), with `35%` reporting no change and `1%` a decrease. The one gradient pointing the other way: `19%` of businesses with more than half of staff using AI reported increased revenue, against `10%` where fewer than half did.

Mechanism
Not a deployment. A population-level measurement of whether AI adoption reaches the top line, with a dose-response cut - share of staff using AI - that is the closest thing in this log to a causal gradient rather than a self-serving attribution.
Why this shape
Counter-evidence tagged to S2, the shape most of this log's positive findings sit in. It does not contradict S1: widely reported productivity and time savings are precisely the velocity shape, and `56%` reporting a productivity gain is consistent with everything S1 in this log. What it contradicts is the assumption that velocity converts - at population scale, more than three quarters of adopters say it has not, yet. The `19%` versus `10%` dose-response is the reason not to read this as a flat null: depth of deployment, not adoption as a binary, is where any revenue effect lives. That is the same shape as the FRBSF bank-lending result logged 2026-09-25.
