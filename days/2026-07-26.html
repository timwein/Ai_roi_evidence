Albertsons Companies Grocery / Retail · US S2 — Share⚠ Counter-evidence

On its Q1 FY2026 call (23 Jul 2026) Albertsons reaffirmed AI as "foundational" and guided FY26 capex of $1.9-2.0bn toward store modernization and AI capabilities, but disclosed no AI-attributed revenue metric. The 10% Ask AI basket-size lift CEO Susan Morris put on the record on the 7 Jan 2026 call was not repeated. Digital sales grew 13%, not attributed to AI.

Mechanism
Ask AI conversational search in the Albertsons/Safeway/Vons/Jewel-Osco mobile apps surfaces complementary items mid-search, expanding the basket; household-level personalized offers on top of ~tens of millions of loyalty households. The mechanism is real and the January number was specific; what is missing is any repeat measurement.
Why this shape
S2, and counter-evidence to it. Basket expansion inside an existing customer base buying existing groceries in existing stores - absent the AI, these shoppers buy groceries from somebody. Zero-sum at industry level, GDP-neutral. Logged as counter-evidence because the only quantified revenue-side AI metric this company had ever disclosed vanished from the next disclosure while AI capex was reaffirmed and expanded. Four named enterprise AI priorities were given (digital CX, merchandising intelligence, labor optimization, supply chain optimization); three of the four are cost-side, and the revenue-side one carried no number.

S&P 500 (510 firms, 2016-2025) - MIT FutureTech / CMU study Cross-sector · US S2 — Share⚠ Counter-evidence

Peer-style study of AI adoption in 510 S&P 500 firms, scored from SEC 10-K language: in 2025 only 11% had AI "deeply integrated into their business processes", plus a further 10% "using AI in the production of goods and delivery of services", up from 5% in 2022. The technology sector "accounts for two-thirds of deeply integrated enterprise adoption", with only "slowly accelerating adoption among non-technology firms". Profitability follows a J-curve from no adoption to deep adoption, but the authors "observe no differences in capex or productivity".

Mechanism
Not a deployment finding - a measurement of the population this log samples from. Adoption is scored off 10-K language, which sits under liability for materially false statements, so it is a harder measure than survey self-report. The finding that matters here is the denominator: two-thirds of genuinely deep enterprise AI adoption sits inside the technology sector, i.e. inside the population METHOD excludes as subjects.
Why this shape
Counter-evidence, tagged to S2 because S2 is where the bulk of revenue-attribution claims live, but it cuts against S3 equally. A J-curve in profitability with no measured productivity or capex difference is a margin story, not a top-line story; the paper finds no top-line effect to report. Directly bears on why this log's positive column is empty: the non-tech incumbents it tracks are precisely the slow-adopting tail, so scarce revenue disclosure may reflect thin real adoption rather than adoption without payoff. Those are very different worlds and this study does not separate them.
