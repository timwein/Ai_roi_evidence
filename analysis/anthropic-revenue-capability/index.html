What would make this wrong

  1. Identification. The time-only trend fits better (R² 0.983; ARR doubling ≈ 3.1 mo) than either capability model — revenue is diffusion on top of capability. These fits answer "if the historical revenue-per-capability elasticity persists," nothing stronger.
  2. Demand saturation. $4T ARR ≈ 3× today's entire enterprise-software market. The 190s assume AI revenue substitutes for a meaningful share of the global wage bill — maybe what a 25-work-month METR horizon means, but far outside these intervals, which carry sampling uncertainty only.
  3. Compute assumed away, as posed. Anthropic's 2026 growth was repeatedly described as compute-gated, so the fitted slope likely understates the unconstrained relationship — the one bias with a known sign.
  4. Specification. Lagging capability 90 days roughly doubles the central estimates; dropping the tracker point trims ~15–20%. Spec choice moves the answer ~2×; the PIs already dwarf that.
  5. Measurement. Run-rate ≠ GAAP revenue (one reported rival-memo challenge of ~$8B; independent estimates roughly corroborate the company figures). Epoch CIs are ±2–3 pts; METR CIs at the top span ~2×; the public Claude 5 family has no METR value yet, so the METR series tops out at Opus 4.6.
  6. ETA risk. Dates assume the linear 14–15.5 pts/yr regime holds. A third-party breakpoint analysis of the restricted Mythos Preview argues for ~67 pts/yr; if that reaches public releases, ETAs compress 2–4×.
